# DEAD CURRENT — Phase 6: Vertical Slice Plan ("The Wrong Characteristic" at Pointe Sombre)

Status: **PLANNED, NOT STARTED, AWAITING ANTHONY'S APPROVAL** (VS-00, 2026-09-30). Nothing below is built. No Phase 6 code, map, asset, quest, item, or dialogue exists. Implementation begins at VS-01 only after Anthony approves or changes this plan.

## Mission

Phase 6 answers two questions.

1. **Does DEAD CURRENT work as an actual game?** A polished 60–90 minute miniature of the whole game (`LongTermPlan.txt` §22–§23), in which the accepted systems combine into one coherent RPG experience:

   **Explore → notice → investigate → talk → use the build → fight / avoid / persuade → read the place → enter a dungeon → form an interpretation → decide with your hands → watch the world change → it persists**

2. **Are we now able to make more DEAD CURRENT without every location needing bespoke engineering?** Several content cells built from one shared grammar: a modular settlement kit (Tier B), a real procedural shoreline recipe (Tier C), a hand-authored focal layer (Tier A), per-cell ownership contracts, independent critics, and Anthony's acceptance, with production metrics that show whether later cells got cheaper **without feeling cheaper**.

The production principle for the whole phase:

**Procedural systems make the hill. Designers deliberately place the thing worth finding.**

The target feeling:

"Pointe Sombre is a real place with real people, the light went dark for four reasons, I found out on my own terms, I changed it with my own hands, and the whole harbor saw what I did."

# 1. Player-Facing Promise

The player arrives at dusk in a Current storm aboard the *Ida*. From the deck they see a light burning on the wrong headland and, beyond it, a lighthouse with no light at all. The *Ida* turns for the wrong light and strikes the reef. In the harbor, Captain Varga will not sail while the point is dark.

Over the next hour the player:

- walks a small, dense island where the dark tower is always in view
- meets the keeper who lied, the trader who does the arithmetic, the wreckers who "only held the lantern", a father whose children draw the pattern in chalk, and a copper buyer who isn't one
- finds a clean cut in the lamp's feed and follows it into the rock
- gets into the Authority vault under the lighthouse one of four ways, depending on who they are and what they found
- crosses live water to a panel that says **SECTION 14 — SEVERED — RESYNC PENDING**, finds Liv's note, and learns she pulled the card and a boy drowned in the dark that followed
- watches the storm pass and a Compact Warden arrive with a card and a deadline
- decides the light **with their hands**: seat the card, light it by hand, throw the feed to the town, flood the vault, or leave it as Liv left it
- carries evidence (or doesn't) into the net loft, where the island decides whom to blame
- steps out into the second storm, at night, and sees what they made

Save anywhere; the result persists.

## 3.2 The prologue decision: option 3 (stays optional; the slice stands alone)

`PROLOGUE_POSTED_FROM_THE_SHORE.md` does **not** become part of Phase 6 and is **not** a prerequisite. The slice uses the standalone fallback the narrative docs already specify: Liv's letter is on board the *Ida* and Mara asks "Who is she to you?" on the crossing.

Reasons:

- **Coherence.** The slice must play standalone regardless: for testers, for critics, for a demo build, and for any player who never saw the shore. So the standalone path is required work either way. The prologue would add a second path, not replace one.
- **Narrative value is mostly preserved.** The two prologue beats the slice depends on (the tie choice and the watch reveal) are already written into the crossing (`SLICE_DIALOGUE.md` §1, `crossing_tie`, `watch`). What is lost is Mara's admission, the promise earned on the shore, and the watch's page on the door. They are good, and they belong to a connection milestone.
- **Cost and risk.** The prologue edits accepted content: `mara_intro` (Shore Watch's own dialogue), a new quest on `Lvl_Boathouse`, three new actors, and new variants on accepted inspectables. It is exactly the kind of edit to a shared generator that blocks parallel work, and it puts accepted Shore Watch behavior at risk during the phase that most needs a stable baseline.
- **Technical.** It is the only thing in the slice that needs map-to-map travel carrying player state across worlds (§6, capability 4). Nothing else in Phase 6 needs it.
- **It does not help prove the slice.** Neither of Phase 6's two questions is answered better by it.

**What Phase 6 does to keep the prologue cheap later:** every slice id that the prologue also uses is the same id (`liv_letter`, `player.tie.*`, `watch.revealed`, `watch.mara_travelling`, `shore.promise_told`). Every prologue-dependent line has the neutral fallback the dialogue already defines. The later prologue milestone is then additive: build the prologue beats on the shore, add a one-way chapter transition, and the slice reads the flags it already knows. That milestone is proposed as **Phase 6.5, "Connection"**, after slice acceptance, and is **not** planned here.

# 5. Map and World Architecture

## 5.1 The recommendation

**One new persistent map, `/Game/Maps/Lvl_PointeSombre`, not World Partition.** The playable exterior is small (target 400–500 m by 300–400 m, about 0.15 km², §5.5). Interiors are **cells inside the same map**, placed outside the exterior's playable bounds and reached through portals that fade, move the player, and fade back (§6, capability 1). There is **no level travel inside the slice.**

`Lvl_Boathouse` stays exactly as accepted.

Why this is the smallest robust architecture, checked against the code:

- `UDCWorldStateSubsystem` and `UDCPersistentRegistry` are **world** subsystems and the quest log and build live on the player pawn. `UGameplayStatics::OpenLevel` destroys all of them. Map travel would need a carry-over mechanism for flags, quests, inventory, and build, and a per-map store for persistent actors (containers, deaths, pickups) that one save file does not have today (`UDCSaveGame::WorldActors` is one map's list). That is a save-format change. In-map cells need **none** of it.
- `ADCConditionalPresence` targets must be in the same level as the rule. Streaming sublevels for interiors would split gameplay actors from their rules (the Landing Stage already hit this and kept gameplay actors in the persistent map).
- Save/load already restores the player transform, so a save made inside the vault loads inside the vault.
- One world means one discovery list, one flag set, one registry: the slice's quest continuity is the same as Shore Watch's.

Rejected:

| Option | Rejected because |
| --- | --- |
| World Partition | No evidence: a 0.15 km² map with interiors does not need streaming grids, data layers, or HLODs. It would change the map-generation scripts, tests, and cook for no measured gain. |
| Separate maps per interior with `OpenLevel` | Needs cross-map persistent-actor state and a `SaveVersion` bump; loses presence across maps; loading hitches at every door. |
| Streaming sublevels for interiors | Cross-level references break presence rules and portals; unloaded persistent actors cannot be saved or restored. Kept only for **stateless** dressing and biome output (§5.6). |
| Interiors physically embedded 1:1 in their shells | The vault would have to fit under the modeled rock; the stair would be a long empty climb; every shell would need a matching interior. Costs layout time and performance for no player value. |

**Why the lamp room stays in place:** its windows and gallery look out over the island, and in B9 the keeper's silhouette on the gallery is seen from the quay. An abstracted lamp room would show the wrong view. The stair between the base and the lamp room is the only part abstracted.

**Why the vault and the loft are abstracted:** neither has windows onto the island; both are authored as closed spaces; each has an owner, a test file, and review views of its own.

Cell placement rules, decided with evidence in VS-04: interior cells sit where the exterior's height fog, sky light, and 2D ambience do not reach them (or are cancelled locally), are enclosed so the sun cannot leak in, and are beyond every exterior sightline and the far clip. Each interior cell has its own post-process volume, lighting, and ambience, the way the boathouse interior already does.

| Portal | From → to | Conditions | On use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wheelhouse door, "Tell Varga about the light" | crossing → quay (one-way) | none | `SetWorldFlag sombre.reef_struck`; card text during the fade: the strike |

| Lower door, "Pry the jam" / "Go in" | sea cliff ↔ vault lower gallery | variant 1 `SkillAtLeast Skill.Engineering 2`; variant 2 `WorldFlag sombre.vault_opened` | variant 1: `SetWorldFlag sombre.vault_opened` |
| Conduit, "Crawl in" | cable hut ↔ vault cable gallery | none (it is found by exploring) | `SetWorldFlag sombre.vault_opened` |
| Loft stair, "Go up to the loft" / "Go down" | quay ↔ net loft | none | "Go down", first variant, only when `sombre.meeting_done` and not `sombre.night_quay_seen`: `SetWorldFlag sombre.night_quay_seen`. The player arrives on the night quay facing the tower, and that flag's rising edge starts the Line outcome's one-time prelude (§6 #2). Otherwise nothing. |

A locked portal shows its locked text ("The hatch is locked. There's a keyhole, and fresh scratches round it.") instead of moving the player. This is how the slice does **condition-driven locked access**: no separate locked-door class.

A portal's uses are ordered **variants**, first match wins, each with its own conditions, verb, and consequences. This is the same grammar as inspect variants. The lower door and the loft stair above need it.

## 5.4 The crossing (B0) and arrival (B1)

- New game starts on the *Ida*'s deck at dusk in `sombre.storm`. The deck is walkable (hidden collision under NoCollision art, the Landing Stage pattern). It does not move; the storm is carried by wind, rain, lightning, spray on the rail, and a passing wave material on the water.
- On the deck: **Liv's letter** (inspectable "Read": `GiveItem liv_letter`, `SetWorldFlag watch.mara_travelling`; tie-neutral text from the prologue doc, P1), a chart pinned in the lee of the wheelhouse (a one-screen recap: the Authority Shore, the Narrows, Pointe Sombre), and **Mara** at the rail (`sombre_mara` `crossing_tie`, `crossing`, `watch`, `third`).
- The **false light** burns on the west headland ahead; beyond it the dark tower shows only in lightning.
- The wheelhouse door is the crossing's exit: "Tell Varga about the light." The player is too late. Fade; the card text reads the strike; `sombre.reef_struck`; the player is on the quay beside the moored, pumping *Ida*. Presence has already moved Mara to the quay (scene cut, §6).
- The *Ida* on the deck and the *Ida* at the quay are two placements of the same art (presence), not two vessels.
- Fallback (only if the deck scene cannot be made to read): a story card over a fixed view from the deck. Anthony decided the crossing is played, so the fallback needs his sign-off.

## 5.5 Compressed geography

**Believable is not the same as realistic distance.** Pointe Sombre must feel bigger than it is.

Compass sketch (not engine units; the map frame is fixed in VS-04 and recorded in `technical_architecture.md`):

           . . . . . . . . . . . open lake . . . . . . . . . . . .
         .        CABLE HUT ~~~ mussel lines run out ~~~            .
        .       (Authority mast)\                                    .
       .      north path ________\______ tower rock ___               .
      .      /                                         [LIGHTHOUSE]    .   the Pointe
 W  [WEST HEADLAND]     SETTLEMENT (store + loft,        base room      E
    false-light post     Odette's cottage, Leclair       lower door in
    and hide             porch, Pruitts' shed,           the sea cliff
      \ reef causeway    smokehouse, net racks)            /
       \                        \___ QUAY ____ harbor ____/
   [ASHLAND GREY]                  the Ida;  Hale's berth later
    stern on the reef

Walking-time targets (at the player's normal walk speed; measured, not guessed, by the route-timing report in VS-08):

| Leg | Target | What fills it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quay → Marthe's store | 10–15 s | the *Ida* pumping, net racks, a dues chit on a door |
| Quay → tower base | 35–50 s | the tower in view the whole climb; Remy's marker just off the path |
| Settlement → cable hut | 25–40 s | the mast seen over the ridge; the hut's loose door heard before it is seen |
| Tower → cable hut | 15–25 s | a cut stair down the north side; the mussel lines visible from above |
| Quay → west headland | 45–60 s | the false-light post in view across the harbor; the Pruitts' shed on the way |
| Headland → *Ashland Grey* stern | 20–30 s | reef rocks at low water; the hull looming |

Rules for the World Architect (VS-07, VS-08):

- **The tower pulls.** It is visible from every exterior cell and from the crossing, and it is never behind the player for long on a main route.
- **Landmarks overlap.** From the quay the player can see the tower (NE), the false-light post (W), and the *Grey*'s hull (SW) at once. From the tower gallery, the whole island.
- **Optional places sit just off natural routes** (the cable hut below the north path; Remy's marker off the tower path; the *Grey* past the headland).
- **Activity is spatial storytelling:** where people stand, what they are working on, what is stacked outside whose door.
- **Quiet is deliberate.** The north path and the reef causeway are intentionally sparse, and they are short.
- **No leg is longer than about 60 s without something to notice** (a sightline, a sound, a person, a clue, a small discovery; VS-20 list).
- **Do not add travel time to look big.** If a stretch feels empty, shorten it before filling it.

## 5.7 Save, load, travel, and compatibility

- **No `SaveVersion` bump expected.** No new save field. The slice is one world; saves record `MapPackage` and F9 already reopens the saved map.
- **Player transform** inside any cell is saved and restored as now.
- **Persistent world state, quest continuity, flags, discoveries** are single-world, exactly as on the shore.
- **One save slot** is shared by both maps (`DeadCurrent`). F5 in the slice overwrites a shore save. Loading a shore save from inside the slice opens `Lvl_Boathouse`, and the reverse; both are tested (`Map.Sombre.CrossMapLoad`). Separate slots are out of scope.
- **Old Phase 5 saves** load on `Lvl_Boathouse` exactly as today (existing `LegacySave`, `LandingStageSaves`, `LandingStagePlayerSave` stay green).
- **Mara's placement** follows the quest stage through presence (§7); nothing about her is saved on its own.
- **Respawn:** today a dead player respawns at the PlayerStart, which in the slice is on the crossing deck. After `sombre.reef_struck` that is wrong. VS-04 tries the existing-system answer first (a presence rule moves the PlayerStart to the quay, then to the tower base once the vault is open). If the game mode does not honor a moved start, the smallest fix is a first-match conditioned respawn point, recorded as a capability change.
- **Packaged build:** the slice map becomes `GameDefaultMap` (default; parked for Anthony in §16.1). `Lvl_Boathouse` stays cooked, loads from its saves, and launches with `Tools\PlayTest.bat Lvl_Boathouse`. `EditorStartupMap` is unchanged.

The slice documents are built **as written**, except for these continuity fixes, each the smallest change, each recorded in `SLICE_DIALOGUE.md`'s change list and shown to Anthony at Checkpoint B:

1. **Dell's wet boots.** "It hasn't rained since the storm" contradicts a slice that opens in a storm. 3. **"Force: Tem keeps a shotgun"** is cut: it needs a ranged NPC weapon and a friendly-to-hostile switch. The slice's fight is on the *Grey* (§8.7).

8. **Pickups that set flags** (`false_lantern` → `false_light_taken`; `liv_note` → `liv_note_found`) use the inspectable-plus-presence pattern (§6 #13).
9. **Copper Buyer** (listed in `QUEST_ARCS.md` §2.5 as trimmed) is carried entirely by Sigrun's dialogue and flags. No quest asset.

## 9.2 The kit

A small vocabulary, composed by data, not a unique asset per building.

- **Shell modules** (authored once as simple meshes or from Geometry Script, with triplanar `M_DC_Surface` instances so scale does not stretch): wall 1 m / 2 m / 4 m, wall with door, wall with window, gable end, corner post, floor/deck slab, lean-to roof, pitched-roof half, porch deck, stair.
- **Skins** (material instances, not new meshes): tarred timber, grey weathered siding (new CC0 wood texture), corrugated steel (existing), rust paint trim (existing), whitewash plaster (existing), concrete (existing).
- **Trim and attachments** (library): corrugated sheets, pier poles as posts, beams, electrical boxes and wire, lantern mounts, shutters, a ladder, rope, floats, net racks, barrels, crates, drums, pallets, buckets, generic skiffs.
- **Compositions** (data, `Tools/Kits/great_lakes_settlement/*.json`): a structure is a footprint, a wall skin per side, openings, a roof type, a porch flag, and a list of attachments. `kit.compose(spec)` places the pieces with a tag `Kit:<PieceId>` and `Structure:<Id>`.

Structures the kit must build, each visibly distinct: Marthe's store (with the net loft's outside stair and gable), the net loft interior shell, Odette's cottage, the Leclair house front and porch, the Pruitts' salvage shed (open-sided), the smokehouse, harbor sheds (2), the cable hut, the tower base room, the *Ida*'s deck furniture and wheelhouse front, Hale's cutter's deck furniture.

**Expected bespoke structural assets:** the lighthouse tower shell, the *Ida*'s hull, Hale's cutter's hull (or the *Ida*'s hull re-trimmed), the *Grey*'s stern. Those are vessels and one landmark, not buildings.

| Share of structural placements that are kit pieces | ≥ 75% |
| Bespoke structural assets (buildings) | **0**; vessels and the tower ≤ 4 |
| Visual critic: "any two buildings read as copies" | no blocker finding |

# 10. Tier C: the Great Lakes Rocky Shoreline Recipe

## 10.1 The success condition

**An agent can define a shoreline zone in data and receive a believable environmental baseline without hand-placing every rock and branch.**

Not "we have PCG".

## 10.2 What it is

- **A recipe** (`Tools/Biomes/great_lakes_rocky_shore.json`): layers, each with a mesh set (library), density per m², clustering, scale and yaw ranges, slope limits, distance-from-waterline band, a seed offset, collision policy, and cull distance. Layers: shore stones along the waterline; gravel and pebble clusters; dark rock outcrops (medium); driftwood (the migrated `DriftWoodPack` low-poly variants plus the three CC0 pieces already in use); dead branches; sparse shoreline grass (a CC0 or library grass, checked against the palette); mud and wet-sand decal variation; maritime litter (rope, floats, a tire, a crate board, a washed-up drum).
- **Zones** (`Tools/Biomes/zones/<cell>.json`, owned by each cell's owner): polygons or boxes in map space with a recipe, a seed, and a density scale.
- **Exclusions**, two kinds:
  - **authored**: `BiomeExclude` boxes placed by cell scripts: doors and portals, interaction approaches, NPC standing spots and patrol areas, the *Grey*'s combat area, route corridors, sightline corridors to the landmarks, persistent actors
  - **automatic**: a radius around every `IDCInteractable`, `ADCLocationVolume` entry edge, NPC, portal, and PlayerStart
- **The tool** (`Tools/EditorScripts/biome/scatter.py`): seeded placement, ground traces against the map's terrain, rejection by rule and exclusion, output as **Hierarchical Instanced Static Mesh** components on one actor per zone in `Lvl_PointeSombre_Biome`, plus a manifest (`Tools/Biomes/out/<map>.json`: per zone and layer, instance counts and a transform hash).
- **Collision:** Tier C is NoCollision (it cannot block traces, bullets, or navigation). Large rocks that should block are Tier B and placed by hand.
- **Gameplay-critical objects are never procedural.** If a generated rock or branch becomes narratively important, it is promoted into the cell script and excluded from the recipe.

## 10.3 PCG or scripted HISM?

Unreal's PCG framework is the long-term candidate. VS-06 opens with a **timeboxed spike** (one working day) and adopts PCG for the recipe **only if** all four hold:

1. it regenerates headless inside `Tools\RebuildContent.bat`
2. the same seed gives the same transforms (hash-equal) on two runs
3. it honors the authored and automatic exclusions
4. the packaged build pays no generation cost at runtime (the result is saved)

Otherwise the recipe is the scripted HISM scatter above, which meets all four by construction and uses the same recipe and zone data, so moving to PCG later changes the tool, not the content. The decision and its evidence go in `technical_architecture.md`.

## 10.4 Acceptance of the recipe

| Measure | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Deterministic | two full rebuilds give identical manifests |
| Exclusions | `Map.Sombre.BiomeExclusions`: zero instances inside any exclusion; zero within the automatic radii |
| Reuse | the same recipe fills ≥ 3 cells' zones (cable hut, headland, harbor shore) with only zone data added |
| Agent test | a new cell's zone is added in ≤ 20 lines of data, with no tool change (the headland's in VS-16, and the VS-20 cell's if one is built) |
| Share | ≥ 80% of Tier C instances in the slice placed by the recipe |
| Cost | same-session A/B, biome on vs off: ≤ 0.5 ms average |
| Look | the visual critic finds no "procedural" or "floating" blocker in the shore views |

# 12. Asset Strategy

## 12.1 Tier rules (enforced)

- **Tier A** (focal: the player studies, remembers, fights, talks to, or decides about it): owned pack → CC0 → Meshy → custom. Close-range review views.
- **Tier B** (the kit): reuse first; a new module is recorded with where it is used.
- **Tier C** (connective): the recipe. **No Meshy credits for Tier C.** NoCollision.
- **Before any generation: search the library** and record the search in the cell spec's Tier A row.

| Direction-finding loop on its turntable | Geometry Script (a ring on a turntable) → Meshy | 0–40 |

| Feed box with cut, folded cable ends | library electrical box + a small cut-ends mesh | 0–30 |

| Named NPC bodies ×8 | §12.3 | — |

**Proposed Meshy stop ceiling for Phase 6: 350 credits** (balance 437). Approving this plan approves the ceiling. What the ceiling means:

- **It is an absolute stop limit, not a budget to spend.** Reaching it is a stop condition, not a goal.
- **Actual spend should be much lower.** Every row above tries the library, the modular kit, Geometry Script, or composition first. The estimates in the table are worst cases, and several rows are expected to cost 0.
- **Every generation needs its cell spec's Tier A justification** before it runs: what the player does with the object, and which library searches failed.
- **Tier B and Tier C never consume Meshy credits.**
- Spend is logged the same day and reported per cell (§14).

## 12.3 Named NPCs (the largest art risk)

Eight named people on one pack body invites the "twins" problem Anthony already flagged once. Default for the build tasks: `Survival_Character` with per-NPC costume tints and a distinct silhouette per person from library props (hat, apron, oilskin, a coil of rope, a Compact coat), plus the accepted one-piece placeholder approach where a body reads wrong. **VS-11 runs a bounded spike** on two better options at low-spec cost: MetaHuman characters at their lowest LODs, and a rigged generated body. It brings captures and frame-time numbers to Checkpoint B, where Anthony chooses. Until then, bodies are placeholders and are judged as placeholders.

## 12.4 Music

None by default. The storm, the hum, and silence carry the slice, as on the shore. A single end-card sting is `[P]`, added only if Checkpoint D asks for it.

## 12.5 Provenance

Every imported or generated asset gets a row in `Design/art_pipeline.md` the same day. Pack migration uses `Tools\ImportPackAssets.ps1` (1K textures, 12 MB package ceiling).

# 17. Stop Conditions

Stop and ask Anthony only when:

- a creative decision outside Anthony's recorded decisions and this plan's defaults blocks progress
- a save-format change, a `SaveVersion` bump, or a renamed shipped id would be needed
- a new C++ capability beyond §6 would be needed (with the content that proves it)
- the atmosphere spike needs `ADCConditionalAtmosphere` (tell, then build: it is pre-approved in principle but must be recorded)
- the work would enter Phase 7 or anything in §3.4
- the Meshy stop ceiling (350) would be exceeded, or a generation lacks its Tier A justification
- the crossing cannot be made to read as a played scene (the fallback needs his sign-off)
- a perf gate fails (§20)
- a builder would have to edit another owner's files, or uncommitted edits by someone else sit in files a task needs
- two architectures with different long-term consequences cannot be settled from the project's rules

# 20. Performance Gates

- **Same-session A/B only.** Each gate captures the previous gate's build and the current build back to back on the same binaries and machine, and compares average frame time over the same views. The ~54 FPS baseline shortfall is deferred and is not a gate.
- **Gates:** 0 blockout (VS-04), 1 greybox + recipe (VS-08), biome on/off (VS-06), NPC population (VS-11), storm (VS-19), finished slice (VS-21), final (VS-24).
- **A material regression** is more than **+1.0 ms** average between consecutive gates, any single view more than **+2 ms** over its previous value, the biome's on/off cost over **0.5 ms**, or the finished slice's average more than **1.0 ms** above `Lvl_Boathouse`'s in the same session. Any one stops the phase for a look.
- `Lvl_Boathouse` itself must not regress (the Phase 5 rule, re-run at VS-24).
- Budgets watched in the manifest: primitives and draw calls per view, dynamic shadowed lights per cell (target ≤ 4 visible at once), texture-pool warnings (none), HISM instance totals.

# 22. Human Acceptance Walkthrough (Checkpoint E, 60–90 minutes)

Delete `Saved\SaveGames\DeadCurrent.sav` (the checklist says where a backup of the shore save is). Launch `Tools\PlayTest.bat Lvl_PointeSombre`. Choose a build on **B** before stepping off the deck (suggested first run: 2 points in one skill of your choice; a second run later can use another).

1. **The crossing (≈5).** Read the letter. Talk to Mara; answer her question however you like. Look ahead: which light is wrong? Tell Varga. *Did the crossing feel played, not watched?*
2. **The harbor (≈5).** Talk to Varga. Look around the quay before going anywhere. F5. *What did you want to go and look at first, and why?*
3. **The settlement (≈15).** Talk to everyone who will talk. Find the children's chalk. Visit Remy's marker. *Which people did you remember? Did anyone feel like a quest dispenser?*
4. **The tower (≈8).** Climb it. Read the lamp room. *Did you know where to go next without being told?*
5. **Getting in (≈5–10).** Get into the vault however your character can. *Was there more than one way you could see?*
6. **The vault (≈15–20).** Cross the lower gallery. Read everything. Find Liv's note. *Did it feel like a dungeon rather than a room with clues? Where was the danger?*
7. **Hale (≈5).** Come out. *Did you notice the harbor had changed before anyone told you?* Talk to Hale.
8. **The headland and the *Grey* (≈10–15).** Find out who shows the lantern: take it as evidence, or get Dell to confess if your build allows. Deal with the wrecker however you like. *Did your build give you different options here?*
9. **Deciding (≈5–10).** Decide the light with your hands. *Did you understand what you physically changed? Did the decision feel earned?*
10. **The loft (≈5–10).** Call the island. Bring what you carry, or don't. *Whose side did you end up on, and did it feel like yours?*
11. **The second storm (≈3–5).** Go down the loft stair onto the night quay. Watch the tower. Talk to Varga. *Did the settlement visibly react? (On the Line: did you catch the three seconds before the light came right?)*
12. **Save and load.** F5 on the quay, quit, relaunch, F9. Everything as it was.
13. **A second outcome (optional, ≈15).** F9 to the `knows` save provided, decide differently, go to the quay. *Was it materially different?*

Overall questions:

- Did Pointe Sombre feel like a real place?
- Was there regularly something worth noticing?
- Did you understand where to go without feeling led by the nose?
- Did your build give you meaningfully different options?
- Did any location feel procedural or generic?
- Did the world feel bigger than it actually is?
- Did any space feel empty merely because we were preserving realistic distance?
- Which characters did you remember?
- Did anything feel like Fallout imitation rather than DEAD CURRENT?
- **Does this feel like a real game?**

# 23. Definition of Done

Phase 6 cannot be accepted unless:

- the full 60–90 minute slice is playable from the crossing to the end card
- exploration, investigation, and multiple RPG approaches matter (tests and Checkpoint E)
- every build check in §8 functions and a zero-investment character can finish every outcome
- the lighthouse and vault work, including all four entries
- False Light works by both approaches: evidence, and confession via `[Persuasion 2]` or `[Survival 2]` (VS-16, `Map.Sombre.FalseLight`)
- every physical resolution path works, with mutual exclusion
- the settlement visibly reacts in the second storm for each outcome; on the Line outcome, the pattern prelude plays once as the player steps from the loft onto the night quay, and never on load (VS-19, `Map.Sombre.SecondStorm`)
- world state persists through F5, divergence, F9, and relaunch at every stage
- old saves (Phase 5, and Anthony's own) still load
- every existing test passes; every required behavior in the §19 matrix is covered by a meaningful new test, and all of them pass (the count is reported, not targeted)
- new gameplay C++ is limited to the three approved capabilities and the scene-cut signal, each built just in time by its first content (§6), plus `ADCConditionalAtmosphere` only if VS-04's spike required it and that was recorded
- Meshy spend is under the 350 stop ceiling, and every generation has its Tier A justification (§12.2)
- `RebuildContent.bat` twice gives identical results
- the Development Win64 package cooks; both production maps smoke-load; no missing cooked assets; no editor-only references
- no unexplained frame-time regression (§20)
- the settlement kit is proven (§9.3), the rocky-shoreline recipe is proven (§10.4), and procedural placement obeys the exclusions
- at least seven cells used the common contract
- the VS-20 fresh-agent test either built a worthwhile real cell from spec + kit + recipe + existing grammar with no new C++, or the report records why no worthwhile cell qualified
- at least one package ran in parallel on isolated ownership and merged cleanly
- independent gameplay and visual reviews happened and every finding was answered
- the production report says whether later cells got cheaper without feeling more generic
- Anthony completed the full playtest and **explicitly accepted Phase 6**

Then stop. Do not start Phase 7.
