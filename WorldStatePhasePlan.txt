## Mission

Prove that **choices visibly alter locations and NPC behavior** (`LongTermPlan.txt` §22, Phase 5).

The loop is:

**Resolve Shore Watch one way → walk back past a familiar place → it looks different, and Mara is somewhere else (or is not) → save, quit, load → it is still different**

Phase 5 is not about storing world state. Flags, quest stages, deaths, discoveries, inventories, containers, save/load, conditional dialogue, inspect variants, and conditioned hazards, lights, and sounds already exist and are accepted. What the rule language cannot yet say is **"this actor is here, somewhere else, or not here at all, when these conditions pass."** This phase adds that one capability and spends it on one small place, the Landing Stage, which is also the approved production pilot (`Design/POIs/PRODUCTION_PILOT.md`).

The target feeling is:

"I did something on this shore, and when I come back to the landing, it shows."

# 2. Player-Facing Promise

On `Lvl_Boathouse`, a small timber landing stage stands in the shallows just east of the boathouse, south of the path, on the way the player walks every time. Before Shore Watch is resolved it is a place someone is getting ready to use: a skiff tied alongside, a lantern lit, a crate half packed. After Shore Watch it reads, from the same spot, without any text, as one of:

| State | Derived from (existing ids only) | The stage | Mara |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before resolution (default) | neither flag below | skiff moored, lantern lit, crate half packed (lid off, contents showing), Mara's pack at her lookout | at her lookout, as now |
| Coil route | `WorldFlag shore.relay_recovered` and not `shore.path_cleared` | crate packed and closed and lashed, her pack beside it, skiff loaded, lantern lit | **on the stage**; her lookout is empty |
| Combat route | `WorldFlag shore.path_cleared` | lantern out, skiff cut adrift (out in the channel, a cut line on the cleat), crate open and empty, lid thrown down | stays at her lookout; her pack stays; the lookout crate and her greeting already read the outcome |
| Power cut (either route, additive) | `WorldFlag wreck.power_cut` | the string of bulbs along the stage, which hummed, is dark and silent | unchanged |

Why these exact flags: `shore.path_cleared` is set only when Shore Watch completes by the kill route (`done_killed` OnEnter); `shore.relay_recovered` when it completes by the coil route, or when a coil is handed over after the kill route. Checking combat first means "killed him, then handed over the coil anyway" stays the combat picture (a drifted skiff does not come back). Killing the scavenger and never telling Mara leaves the stage in its default state: Shore Watch is not resolved.

Every state survives F5 → diverge → F9, and a save made before this phase loads with the stage showing whatever those flags already say.

# 3. Scope Lock

# 4. Architecture

## 4.1 Conditional presence

`ADCConditionalPresence` is a rule actor, not a component, because the map scripts spawn plain `StaticMeshActor`s, `DCFlickerLight`s, and `DCFriendlyNPC`s and can set an actor-reference array on another actor in the same level, but cannot reliably add instance components from Python. One rule, many targets.

- `Targets` (actors in the same level). Each keeps its offset from the rule actor's authored transform, captured at BeginPlay. The rule actor is the pivot.
- `States`: ordered `{ StateId, Conditions, bPresent, bMove, Placement }`. The first whose conditions pass wins. If none pass, the targets are at their authored transforms and present. (An unconditioned last state is an explicit default, e.g. "hidden unless".)
- `bMove`: the pivot goes to `Placement` (a world transform); each target goes to `Placement × its offset`. Not moving keeps the authored transform.
- `bPresent = false`: targets are hidden in game and their collision is off, so an interaction trace, a bullet, or a walking player cannot hit them. Nothing is destroyed; the persistent registry and save are untouched.
- **Evaluation context** is the local player's pawn when there is one (so `HasItem`, quest, and build conditions work), otherwise the rule actor, exactly like `ADCConditionalAudio`.
- **When:** at BeginPlay (snap), on `OnRestored` (snap), on world-state `OnChanged`, and on a timer (`CheckInterval`, 0.5 s) that also picks up quest and inventory changes.
- **Deferral** (`bDeferWhileObserved`, default on): a change other than a snap waits while the player is within `ObservedDistance` (default 15 m) of the pivot's current or next placement, or while any target was rendered on screen in the last half second. So Mara does not vanish mid-conversation, and nothing pops in at the player's feet. The pending state applies as soon as neither holds. Tests and tools can force it with `Snap()`.
- **Saves nothing, sets nothing.** No persistent id, no flag, no save field. Old saves and new saves are the same format.
- Getters for tests and review: `GetActiveStateId()` (`NAME_None` for the default), `HasPendingChange()`.
- `IsHidden()`, collision, and transforms of the targets are the observable result.

Rejected alternatives: a component on each target (not authorable from the map scripts without the subobject API, and a group — the skiff and its cargo — would need N copies of the same rule); saving the placement (redundant with the flags, and would break when content changes); a condition-type extension (presence is not a check, it is a consumer of checks).

## 4.2 Restore signal

`UDCWorldStateSubsystem` gains `FSimpleMulticastDelegate OnRestored` and `NotifyRestored()`. `UDCSaveSubsystem::ApplyPendingLoad` calls it after the player and quest log are applied. The review capture calls it after applying a viewpoint's setup. `ADCConditionalPresence` snaps on it. Nothing else changes in save/load. No `SaveVersion` bump.

## 4.3 Where the Landing Stage lives

- Gameplay actors (presence rules and their targets, inspectables, the container, the discovery volume, the lamp-string lights and hum, the deck with collision) are in the persistent map, because presence rules reference their targets and cross-level references are not allowed. They are built by `Tools/EditorScripts/build_landing_stage.py`, a separate file that owns only this POI. `build_boathouse.py` calls it with one hook at the end of `main()`, passing itself as the toolkit (its helpers: `box`, `inspectable`, `cond`, `flicker_light`, `conditional_audio`, `wear_mesh`…). That hook is the whole of the change to the shared map script. This answers the pilot's open question (`PRODUCTION_PILOT.md` §5) in favour of the per-POI script; what broke, if anything, is recorded in the spec.
- Static dressing with no state is in the art level, placed by `dress_landing.py` (tag `LandingDress`, NoCollision). Like the other `dress_*` scripts it is not part of `RebuildContent.bat`, and a content rebuild leaves its actors.
- Collision: only the walkway and deck (walkable) and the crate, skiff, and Mara (interaction). Everything else is NoCollision. The walk from the boathouse's lake side to the scavenger camp (the review route through (2000, −400) → (700, −450)) crosses no new collision: the stage starts at the shore curb (Y −580) and runs south into the water.
- No quest, dialogue, or item asset changes. Inspect text lives in the POI script (Narrative Builder's part of it).

## 4.4 Ids

- New: location `shore.landing_stage`; container `landing.tackle` (a small tackle box, the stage's one loot).
- Reserved by the pilot and not used, because inspectables carry no persistent state: `landing.crate`, `landing.skiff`, `landing.note`. They stay reserved.
- No new flag. No new quest stage. No `SaveVersion` bump.

# 8. Save and Backward Compatibility

- No `SaveVersion` bump. No new save field. No renamed id, flag, stage, or asset.
- The presence result is recomputed from flags on every load, so a save from any earlier version shows the stage in whatever state its flags imply, and the stage is undiscovered until walked onto.
- New persistent ids (`landing.tackle`) and the new location are absent from older saves, which is the existing "added after the save" case: the container keeps its authored contents, the location is undiscovered.
- Verified by `LandingStageSaves` (hand-written version-5 saves) and by the unchanged `LegacySave` test.

# 9. Performance and Regression

- Budget: at most about 30 new actors in the persistent map, two new shadowless point lights (the lantern and one for the lamp string), one looping positional sound. Presence rules tick at 0.5 s and do nothing else between changes.
- Measure the capture run's average frame time before (`Saved/Review/2026-09-29_2246` family, about 18.4 ms) and after WS-05 and WS-10. **A material regression is more than +1.0 ms average**, or any view more than 2 ms slower than its own earlier value; either stops the phase for a look. Otherwise the deferred ~54 FPS issue is left alone.
- As built (WS-05): about 40 small actors plus 8 presence rules (rules render nothing), the lantern light, one bulb light (two more bulbs are glow only), one hum. Over the 30-actor guess; the frame-time check below is what decides.
- As measured (WS-05): the machine was about 11.7 ms slower on 2026-09-30 than on 2026-09-29 for the unchanged pre-Phase-5 map, so comparing against last night's run is meaningless. The check is a same-session A/B instead: the pre-Phase-5 maps (from `532994d`) and the Phase 5 maps captured back to back on today's binaries. Result on the 24 original views: +0.36 ms average (runs 2026-09-30_1047 against _1044); single views move up to ±5 ms either way between runs, so that is inside the noise. A later run at WS-05's commit averaged 2.3 ms under the pre-Phase-5 run. No material regression.
- No new collision on the lake-side walk, the review route, the scavenger's patrol square, or the boathouse door. The firearm's traces, the interaction trace, and navigation are unchanged elsewhere; the deck adds walkable ground only.
