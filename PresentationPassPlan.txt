Anthony's first playtest (2026-09-29) sent it back for fixes (cot, hum and wind levels, Mara's face, the live-water look, the map edge, and a prop material that never compiled); those are in and it awaits a second playtest. **Not accepted.** Phases 1–4 are accepted. ## Mission

Make the existing `Lvl_Boathouse` shore look and sound like DEAD CURRENT, so the clues a player is meant to read can actually be read.

The loop is:

**Walk the same shore → the place reads as cold, maritime, industrial, and strange → the same clues, routes, and builds still work**

No new place, quest, NPC, enemy, item, dialogue line, or lore. Every actor, persistent id, world flag, and inspect variant stays. Art replaces what those actors look like, and sound is added beside them.

The target feeling is:

"I'd stop and look at that even if it didn't glow."

# 3. Art-Layer Decision

`Lvl_Boathouse_Art` is a hand-authored streaming sublevel. `build_boathouse.py` re-links it on every rebuild and never loads it for editing, never destroys its actors, and never saves it.

Why this and not script-placed dressing for everything:

- The persistent map is generated. Dressing that is not a gameplay actor (driftwood, camp scrap, shore stones, the visual hull of the *Tern*) is level art. Putting it in the script would mean a coordinate for every rock, and the next layout tweak would be a Python edit. A separate `.umap` is the file the script does not own.
- Gameplay actors cannot move to that sublevel. Inspectables, containers, the door, the water slab, the damage volume, the sparks, the NPCs, and the pickups are what the map tests find by class, label, and persistent id. They stay in `Lvl_Boathouse`. Their meshes and materials are assigned by the script, from assets under `/Game/Art` and migrated pack paths, so a rebuild restores their look.
- Mood stays in the script too. `build_lighting()` already recreates the sun, sky light, atmosphere, height fog, and post process. A second sun in the art level would double them. The art level does not add a directional light, sky light, height fog, sky atmosphere, or unbound post-process volume.

What the rebuild must do, specifically:

1. Consider only actors in the persistent level when it destroys and rebuilds. `get_all_level_actors()` includes a loaded sublevel. Filtering on the actor's level is the difference between a surviving art layer and an empty one.
2. If `/Game/Maps/Lvl_Boathouse_Art` does not exist, create it once, empty except a sentinel actor tagged `ArtLayerSentinel`, and save that level. On every later run, do not open it.
3. Add it back as `LevelStreamingAlwaysLoaded` on the persistent map after the rebuild, so the game and the map tests load it with `Lvl_Boathouse`.
4. Save the persistent map only.

Proof: a sentinel actor lives only in the art level. `Tools\RebuildContent.bat` runs. The sentinel is still in `Lvl_Boathouse_Art`, and a map test on `Lvl_Boathouse` finds the tag `ArtLayerSentinel`. A second rebuild does not duplicate the sublevel reference.

Collision rule for everything placed in the art level: **NoCollision**, unless a later task writes down why a specific mesh needs simple collision and the map tests are rerun. Hitscan traces WorldStatic and WorldDynamic. Decorative clutter that blocks a trace eats bullets. The scavenger's patrol square (X 2000..2700, Y -350..350), the ridge that hides Mara (Y 550..650), the boathouse doorway, the wheelhouse, the boarding plank, and the live water (X -2020..-980, Y -1780..-1000) stay as the script built them.

Walkable ground, walls, the ridge, and the hull stay the existing blocks. They get real materials. The blocks are the collision. Art meshes dress them. They are not replaced by a landscape.

# 4. Look and Sound

Mood, under the PlayTest cvars (no volumetric fog, no bloom, no Lumen):

- Directional light: low, cool, overcast. The scene reads by light color and intensity, not by bloom.
- SkyAtmosphere: grey-blue, high scattering, no heroic sunset.
- Exponential height fog: a cold grey density and color. Volumetric fog stays off. If scalability 0 strips the fog, the light color and the post-process grade still carry the cold.
- Post process: white balance toward cold, pulled-down saturation, a slight contrast. No DOF, no vignette that depends on a quality level the cvars disable. Auto-exposure stays bounded so the interior and the shore are both readable.
- Palette, used and not canonized: black rock, wet sand, mud, oxidized teal hull paint, rust, faded white, birch grey. No tropical green. No brand color.

Water: a project material on the existing `WaterSurface` slab (unlit or default-lit translucent, dark freshwater, a slow normal). Not the engine Water plugin. `LiveWater` stays an `ADCDamageVolume` on `M_DC_Glow` with `ActiveConditions = [!wreck.power_cut]`. The slab has no collision. The glow mesh hides when the hazard is inactive, which is already how the leads work. A loop on that same active check is the electrical hum. The slab does not disappear.

Clue actors keep their classes, ids, variants, actions, and consequences. The script swaps the static mesh (and, where the mesh has no material of its own, the material). Scale stays in the same footprint the trace already hits, so **E** still finds them. Simple collision, bounds close to the old box.

NPC bodies: `assign_mannequin` points Mara and the scavenger at `Survival_Character` skeletal meshes on the mannequin skeleton, with the existing unarmed anim blueprint. Classes, AI, dialogue, and ids stay. Modules are the pack's cold-weather jacket and jeans. That is a costume, not a decision about Mara's face or history.

Audio that has to follow state uses existing conditions. It does not set flags.

- Pistol: `FireSound` and `DryFireSound` on `DA_Item_Pistol`, already played by `ADCFirearm`. Point them at CC0 shots from the library. No new fire logic.
- Pickup and UI: play a sound from `Interface_And_Item_Sounds` when a pickup is taken and when the inventory opens. Missing assets no-op, the way an empty firearm sound already no-ops.
- Shore bed: looping wind and water lap, placed in the art level, no conditions, no collision.
- Live water: a loop that starts and stops with `ADCDamageVolume`'s existing active check (`!wreck.power_cut`). Same tick that already shows and hides the glow.
- Relay: a cosmetic actor, `ADCConditionalAudio`, that loops while its conditions pass and is silent otherwise. Conditions match the rig's silent variants: the player is not holding `radio_coil`, `shore.relay_recovered` is unset, `boat.scavenger` is alive. Inspecting the rig does not stop the hum. The actor sets nothing.

`ADCConditionalAudio` evaluates the same `FDCGameplayCondition` list the rest of the game uses, against the player when one exists, so `HAS_ITEM` and `ACTOR_DEAD` work. It is presentation, the same kind of actor as `ADCFlickerLight`. No new condition type, no new flag, no save field.

Anything not in the library is CC0 or royalty-free, with the file, author, and URL in the provenance table. If a sound cannot be sourced legally, it is listed as a gap in `Design/art_pipeline.md`. It is not skipped silently and it is not replaced with a ripped clip.

# 5. Assets

Library root: `C:\FO5_AssetLibrary`. Bring only what is placed.

- UE pack assets migrate to the same `/Game/<Pack>/...` path. Never a whole pack.
- Raw FBX, glTF, and textures import through `Tools/EditorScripts/import_art.py` into `/Game/Art/<Source>/<AssetId>/` (`Megascans`, `PolyHaven`, `AmbientCG`, `Meshy`, `Freesound`). Textures 2K max, 1K for small props. Never the 8K Megascans maps at full size. The script is safe to re-run and is registered in `Tools\RebuildContent.bat` ahead of `build_boathouse.py`, because the map references those assets.
