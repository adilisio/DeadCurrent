# DEAD CURRENT — Mystery Ledger (PROVISIONAL)

Status: **PROVISIONAL, 2026-09-29.** A continuity tool for anyone writing quests, inspect text, dialogue, or transmissions. It separates what the player can know from what only looks true, and it lists what must never be answered cheaply. Nothing here is canon until Anthony confirms it.

The Current is **not explained** anywhere in this file, including the author sections. §9 lists constraints any future explanation would have to satisfy, so that nothing written now closes a door Anthony may want open.

## 5. Recurring phenomena and the rules of the Current

The Current's **rules** keep content systemic. Quest writers use these rather than inventing new behavior. Each maps to existing or planned tags (`Damage.Electrical`, `Status.Electrified`, flags).

### 5.1 Rules (PROPOSAL)

1. **It follows water and long conductors.** Strongest along cables, shorelines, wrecks, rails, fences, anchor chains. (Exceptions exist: C1, C3, C11.)
2. **It rises with storms and at night.** The pattern's spacing **tightens before weather**, which is how listeners forecast.
3. **The hum is at sixty cycles**: the frequency of a North American power grid that has not existed for sixty years. Every "hum" in the game is the same tone (the existing relay and beacon hum already match each other).
4. **Signaling devices carry the pattern; working devices repeat their last task.** Lights, radios, beacons, sounders, and bells carry the pattern. Motors, pumps, gates, and ferries do what they were doing when they were left. (The *Tern*'s beacon, taped to TRANSMIT, hums; the automatic ferry runs its schedule.)
5. **Cutting power reduces it locally, never to zero.** "Cut every breaker on the boat. Still feel it through my boots." (existing)
6. **Voices appear only near nodes**, on specific channels, mostly as archive. Unaccounted voices are rare: one per act at most.
7. **Speaking protocol on a node's channel operates that node.** Usually as documented. Sometimes not (C9).
8. **Wildlife:** fish die in rings at a fixed radius; **no birds** near active nodes (existing window line: "No birds"); mayflies rise in rings over live cables; zebra and quagga mussels grow in straight **mussel lines** along live cable routes on the lake bed; sturgeon gather at junctions; animals refuse to cross ice above cables during events.
9. **People:** sleeplessness during storms near nodes (children most); a ringing in the ears listeners call "the hum-ear"; strong compulsion in some listeners to answer. **No mind control, possession, mutation, or madness-as-spectacle.**
10. **It harms only through electricity and through what infrastructure does.** Shock, fire, a light that misleads, a gate that closes, a pump that stops.

### 5.3 What the transmissions contain (for writers)

In rough proportion:

- **~60% Authority storm and traffic archive**: "All vessels, all vessels, Current event in progress, make for nearest shelter", lock schedules, lake levels, section status ("Section fourteen, stand by for resync").
- **~20% live data read in an Authority voice**: present weather from surviving buoys, present vessel positions from transponders. (This is how the relay can say the *Ida*'s name.)
- **~10% archived distress calls** from the collapse week. Real people, sixty years dead. The Mourners' evidence.
- **~10% protocol**: calls and replies between nodes.
- **Rare, unaccounted**: something that is none of the above. One per act at most. Never a full sentence of explanation.

## 6. The reveal ladder

Each rung **answers** a previous question and **raises** a more consequential one. The player climbs most rungs by **doing** (plotting, powering, speaking, opening), not by being told.

| # | Act | The player does | Answer | New question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Prologue | Hears the relay; inspects the *Tern*'s beacon and mast lamp. | Dead equipment is live. | What is powering it? |
| 2 | Prologue | Lays the Sounder Chart against the sounder (existing). | Independent machines share one pattern, and it is a **bearing**. | What is on the other end of the bearing? |
| 3 | Act I | Reads the vault's Authority log. | The Authority knew "Current events" as ordinary hazards and logged them for decades. | Then why did it keep records of a *pattern* inside them, in a separate locked book? |
| 4 | Act I | Powers the vault's direction-finding loop during the storm; plots bearing two on Liv's chart beside her own pencil line. | The pattern comes from a single region beyond the Narrows. | Two bearings are too close together to fix it. Where exactly? And Liv had the same problem. |
| 5 | Act I | Reads the node panel: SECTION 14 — SEVERED — RESYNC PENDING. | The Line did not just fail; this node was **cut by an order**, and it is trying to rejoin. | Who cut it, and what happens when it rejoins? |
| 6 | Act II | Compares the Compact's transponder roster with transmissions. | The Line still **watches** the lakes. | Who is it watching them for? |
| 7 | Act II | Speaks a phrase on the lock channel (or hears someone else do it). | The words **operate** the infrastructure. The taboo was earned. | Then who is speaking the replies that are not in the manual? |
| 8 | Act II | Takes the third bearing at Tall Masts. | The source is **the Four-Mile Crib**. | What is in the Crib, and why is Liv there? |
| 9 | Act II | Compares sounder rolls against lake-level records (Engineering) and sits the Vigil. | The pattern follows the lake's **seiche**, and it **changes when people speak**. | Is it a physical rhythm, or something that uses one? |
| 10 | Midpoint | Hears "Kallio at the Crib" on every relay. | Liv is alive, and she is in the Crib. | Why would she tell everyone? (Later: she didn't.) |
| 11 | Act III | Powers the pumping station; watches the Line cut the squatters' heat to light a flooded hospital. Reads the Severance record and the tables. | The collapse was made permanent **on purpose**, perhaps by no one. The tables are the catastrophe. | If the Line rejoins, whose world does it restore? Who has the right to rewrite that? |
| 12 | Endgame | Watches the pattern change as Liv refuses a request. | Something in the Crib reacts to refusal. | Is it asking? Does answering mean anything? The player must act without knowing. |

**Pacing check:** rungs 1–2 are already built. Rungs 3–5 all live in the vertical slice, which makes the slice a true miniature of the game's investigative structure. No rung is pure exposition: each needs a verb.

## 8. Questions intentionally unanswered at the ending

- What the Current **is**.
- Whether the pattern is **asking**, or only retrying.
- Who entered Holt's code (C6).
- How Liv's private words were aired (C4), and how the player's own words were repeated (C5).
- Whether the old gale log is real (C8).
- Whether the Severance saved the region or doomed it.
- What, if anything, **answering** changes for whatever is on the other end.

Each has evidence on more than one side by the end. The player can hold a well-supported opinion about each. They cannot prove it.

## 9. Author constraints (hidden)

Not an explanation. A fence. Any future explanation Anthony chooses should satisfy all of these, and no content should contradict them:

1. It is **coupled to lake and weather physics** (the seiche period, storms, water).
2. It **uses conductors but does not require them** (C1–C3, C11).
3. It **reacts to human speech and attention**, in limited and inconsistent ways (C9, C10).
4. It is **never demonstrably hostile or benevolent.** Harm follows from what people and infrastructure do.
5. It **predates the collapse by at least a decade**, and possibly by much more (contested, C8).
6. It **does not depend on the Line to exist**, but the Line changes its reach and volume.
7. **No single person** explains it, caused it, or is its key. Not Liv, not Holt, not the player.

## 10. Forbidden cheap answers

Do not write, imply, or foreshadow any of these as *the* answer:

- **The network became conscious**: a rogue AI, a digital god, a machine that wants.
- **Aliens**, or anything from off-world.
- **Literal ghosts**: the dead speaking. (The Mourners may believe it. The game must not confirm it.)
- **A government experiment** that created it, or a cover-up villain who knows everything.
- **A weapon** someone built (the Cutters may believe it).
- **Magic**, crystals, artifacts, ley lines.
- **A simulation** or dream.
- **Nanotechnology**, nanites, swarms.
- **Time travel** or messages from the future (C5 must never be explained as the future).
- **The player is special**: the signal is about the player, the player is a descendant of Holt, the player can hear what others cannot.
- **Liv is the Current**, or became part of it, or it is using her body.
- **Mara is immortal, a ghost, or a construct** (her age question in `STORY_REVIEW.md` must be answered mundanely or left alone).
- **Indigenous spirituality as the explanation.** Anishinaabe and Haudenosaunee peoples are part of this region's present, not a source of mystical answers. See `STORY_REVIEW.md`.
- **One final exposition dump**, in any voice, that explains it.

## 12. Continuity notes

- The collapse is **about sixty years** before the game (canon). The *Tern* grounded "since the lights went out", i.e., on or near the collapse night (in game). The Pattern Desk worked for about a decade before that (PROPOSAL).
- "The Line" is what Authority staff called the network; the name survived in the Local ("Burn the Line") and among listeners. Most island folk just say "the Authority".
- **Never** write a transmission that is a clear, complete message from an intelligence. Fragments, protocol, archive, and at most one uncanny line per act.
- Hum: always the same tone. Pattern: always evenly spaced, tightening before weather.
- Liv's letters and notebook entries are signed **L.** Her handwriting has a distinctive capital **A with no crossbar** (a listener's habit: a crossbar can be misread as a dash in a transcript). The **A** in "HEAR IT TOO" should, when the art pass reaches it, share that hand (PROPOSAL). The match is a Fieldcraft or Survival-style reading once the player carries one of her letters.
