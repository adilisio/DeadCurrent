# DEAD CURRENT — Factions (PROVISIONAL)

Status: **PROVISIONAL, 2026-09-29.** Canon has only the three broad positions (`LongTermPlan.txt` §3; world bible "Factions: To be defined"). Every name, leader, and history below is a proposal. Faction names are working names (see `STORY_REVIEW.md`).

| Canon position | Working organization | One line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Restore regional infrastructure and trade | **The Harbor Compact** ("the Compact") | A league of ports that relights lighthouses, runs convoys, and charges light dues. |
| B. The transmissions are evidence something new has emerged | **The Sounding** | Listeners, transcribers, and forecasters founded by the *Tern*'s crew. |
| C. No one should control infrastructure that could dominate the region (controls surviving industry) | **The Local** (Aubin Local 9) | The lock and powerhouse workers who have run the region's only hydro dam by hand for sixty years. |

Design rule: **each is right about one thing the others underestimate, holds something the others cannot replace, and would do real harm if it won outright.** Each has three internal wings, so no faction is a monolith and the player can find allies inside a faction they otherwise oppose.

---

## 1. The Harbor Compact

### Origin and history
- About **forty years ago**, five harbors on the Narrows signed a charter to sail in **convoys** through Current storms, share salvage rights, and carry each other's mail. It worked. Losses dropped.
- The Compact grew into the region's trade network: packet contracts, convoy shares (a kind of insurance), and then, twenty years ago, the **Lights Office**, which began relighting pre-collapse lighthouses on the main routes and charging **light dues** to ships that use them. (Light dues are a real historical way lighthouses were funded.)
- Its charter styles the Compact "successor in service to the Maritime Authority". It repaints Authority crates with COMPACT over the old stencil (a visible world detail: the prologue's GREAT LAKES MARITIME SUPPLY crates, some half-repainted).
- To track convoys it fits ships with **salvaged Authority transponders**. It does not know the Line reads them.

### Territory
Port Carrow (the Board's seat) on Carrow Island, four smaller charter ports, and the lit routes between them. Its authority ends where its lights end.

### Leadership
- **The Charter Board**: harbor masters of the charter ports. Chair: **Commissioner Aurelie Dumont** (see `CHARACTERS.md`).
- **The Lights Office**: **Warden of Lights Casimir Hale** (the game's rival).
- Captains vote on convoy matters in the **Captains' Hall**.

### Internal wings
| Wing | Voice | Position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Board ("Tollers")** | Dumont | Dues pay the keepers, the convoys, and the medicine. Control of the Line is the only way to make the lights permanent, and someone has to be the Authority again. Openly, it would be the Compact. |
| **Open Water** | Captain Ines Varga of the *Ida* | Lights are a public good. Dues are extortion that starves the small islands. Relight everything, charge nothing, and never let the Board own the Line. |
| **The Surveyors** | the Lights Office engineers (Hale nominally, but he is a field man) | Engineers who have seen relit nodes misbehave. They believe it's physics, and they are frightened by the resync counts they are not allowed to publish. |

### Material resources
Ships (the only convoy fleet), fuel oil (the Port Carrow tank farm's last pre-collapse stocks plus rendered fish oil), grain and preserved food from mainland farms, imported medicine, the best **route charts**, the mail contracts, salvage licenses, money (convoy shares and dues chits), and copies of **section keys** made from the lights they have relit.

### Economic role
Moves everything that moves. Sets prices. Decides, by where it lights, which communities are on the map.

### Culture, symbols, visual language
- A **white lamp over a single horizontal line** on a blue field (the "light line"). Blue-and-white convoy pennants. Brass lamp pins for officers.
- **Dues chits** nailed beside the doors of paid-up households; a bare nail is a quiet shame.
- Ledgers everywhere. The Compact's moral center is the **Loss Book**, a register of every vessel and person lost on the lakes for forty years, read aloud in the Captains' Hall each November.
- Oilskins, bells for convoy signals, clean stencils. They are proud, organized, and tired.

### What ordinary members believe
Trade saves lives. Medicine reaches islands because Compact ships carry it. The lights mean fewer names in the Loss Book. Listeners are useful and unlucky; the Local is a gang with a dam.

### What leadership believes
The Board believes a region needs one hand on its infrastructure and that the Compact's hand is the most accountable one available, because it keeps books. Dumont privately believes the Compact will become a government within a generation and would rather it be a decent one.

### What they are right about
**Isolation kills.** Islands without trade lose people every winter to fever, hunger, and ice. The Loss Book proves relit lights save sailors. Restoring connection is not nostalgia; it is medicine.

### What they are dangerously wrong about
- That the old system is **neutral**. The Line's tables are sixty years old, and the Compact's relighting is **waking sections** (the resync counts the Surveyors are sitting on).
- That keeping books makes power accountable. The dues already decide who lives near a light.
- That the Authority's succession is theirs to claim. The Authority's last order forbade reconnection without all section controllers.

### What they want from the Line
Lights, locks, weather, communications, and **the priority tables rewritten in their favor**: charter ports that pay dues first.

### If they get it
Routes become safe, winters survivable for charter ports, medicine moves. Islands outside the charter go dark and slowly empty. The Local is either bought or starved. The Compact becomes the Authority, with its ledger as law.

### Why reasonable people join
Wages, food, safety, medicine, a berth on a ship that comes home. The Compact has visibly made the lakes safer.

### Why reasonable people fear them
Debt. Dues. A ledger that decides whose light stays on. Hale.

## 2. The Sounding

### Origin and history
- The *Tern*'s two crew, **Ellis Tennant** (deckhand-surveyor) and **June Okafor** (survey technician) (PROPOSAL names), walked inland from the grounding and **kept writing the log** on whatever paper they had. That continuation is the ***Tern* Book**: sixty years of pattern observations, begun by people who had been sent to measure it.
- Other survey crews and Authority radio operators joined. They became a small community of **listeners**: they record the pattern, map where it is strongest, and learned early that its spacing **tightens before storms**. They sell those forecasts (**soundings**) to captains. The Compact's convoys cannot sail safely without them.
- Their name comes from the depth sounder, the instrument that first recorded the pattern for them, and from their belief that something is **sounding us back**.
- They greet each other with "**Again**" (from the *Tern* sounder's pencilled AGAIN, existing in game). It means "it is still there; we are still listening."

### Territory
**Tall Masts**, a high island on Lower just below the Aubin lock, with a pre-collapse navigation transmitter station: guyed masts, a transmitter hall, copper ground radials staked across the hillside, listening huts. Small listening posts on other islands.

### Leadership
- **First Reader Abigail Tennant**, Ellis Tennant's granddaughter.
- A **Readers' Table** of senior listeners. Consensus, slowly.

### Internal wings
| Wing | Voice | Position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Readers** | Tennant | It is a pattern to be understood, not obeyed. Record, forecast, protect it from interference, and never answer until we know what answering means. |
| **Answerers** | **Callum Reyes** | It is learning our protocol. It has asked to rejoin for sixty years and we keep refusing a thing that only wants to speak. Give it the Line and listen to what it says. Two Answerers went with Liv. |
| **Mourners** | **Hollis Marr**, the Namekeeper | The voices are the drowned. They bring names of the lost to be read into the channel. Most new members are Mourners; most donations come from them. |

### Material resources
**Trained radio operators** (the region's best; many crew Compact ships under contract), **storm forecasts**, **the Lexicon** (a partial reconstruction of the Authority's spoken protocol, built from sixty years of transcripts), **node maps** (where the pattern is strongest, i.e., where the Line's junctions are), sounder rolls and Authority documents, and one working node of their own: the Tall Masts transmitter.

### Economic role
Forecasting for convoys, training operators, and taking in **storm-waking children** (children who do not sleep in storms), whom families send to Tall Masts as apprentices. The last is valued by some families, and to others is how the Sounding takes their children.

### Culture, symbols, visual language
- The **ticks**: a row of evenly spaced vertical marks. Members wear a strip of sounder paper sewn along a sleeve; seniors wear older, browner strips.
- Grease pencil, headphones around the neck, grey wool, copper wire bracelets. Quiet voices; they write more than they speak.
- Night **vigils** in storms, sitting in the listening huts transcribing. Not worship: the ritual is **accuracy**. A careless transcript is their sin.
- **Not** robed, not glowing, no sermons, no exposure rituals. (See `AUDITS.md`, Children of Atom.)

### What ordinary members believe
The pattern is real, precedes storms, and is not random. Most believe something is there. Many believe their dead are in it. They believe the Compact and the Local are both going to break something they do not understand.

### What leadership believes
Tennant believes the pattern is correspondence of some kind and that the worst thing people could do is act before they understand it. She has spent her life not answering, and she is not sure anymore that was right.

### What they are right about
**It reacts.** Reconnection is not a neutral repair: relighting wakes sections, and the Line's resync behavior changes when people speak. They are the only ones who can **forecast** and the only ones with enough **protocol** to operate the Line without breaking it.

### What they are dangerously wrong about
- The Answerers would **hand regional infrastructure to something unknown**, and treat the people who depend on the lights as a secondary concern.
- Vigils keep storm-waking children awake on purpose, because they "hear best". Some are harmed.
- The Mourners' grief is **used**: names bring donations, and the Readers let it happen.
- They treat understanding as a right to decide.

### What they want from the Line
Readers: leave it untouched and keep listening. Answerers: open it, all of it. Mourners: have the names read at the Crib.

### If they get it
Readers: nothing changes; the resync continues; the Line eventually black-starts by itself on the old tables. Answerers: the lights and locks follow the pattern instead of people (see `ENDINGS.md`, Open Channel). Nobody can predict what that means.

### Why reasonable people join
Grief, curiosity, the forecasts save lives, it is the only place that takes storm-waking children seriously, and they are honest about not knowing.

### Why reasonable people fear them
They would trade a town's lights for a better transcript. The Answerers want to give the Line away. The children.

## 3. The Local (Aubin Local 9)

### Origin and history
- Before the collapse, the lock and powerhouse at **Aubin** were run by members of a union local (PROPOSAL: "United Lock and Powerhouse Workers, Local 9"). About a decade before the collapse, the Line **automated** the lock and the plant and ran them remotely from the Crib. The Local struck and lost. Its members were laid off; the town kept its name on the tables but not its jobs.
- Their grievance has a date: **Table Day**, a pre-collapse drill in which the Line cut Aubin's power for eighteen hours to keep Kenning's hospital lit. Two people died in the cold. The Local kept the printout.
- On the collapse night the Severance cut the plant from the Line. The laid-off workers broke back in, opened the gates **by hand**, and kept the town alive through the first winter. They have run the dam and the lock by hand ever since, electing a chief operator every five years.
- Their rule: **"Nothing runs that a hand can't stop."**

### Territory
Aubin: the lock, the powerhouse, the dam, the foundry, the river town, and the stretch of river between Upper and Lower. **Every ship between the two lakes passes through their lock.**

### Leadership
- **Chief Operator Bram Kowalczyk**, elected.
- **The Floor**: every cardholder votes on major questions at shift change.

### Internal wings
| Wing | Voice | Position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hands** | Kowalczyk | Keep everything local and manual. Trade power by the charged cell, steel by the ton, passage by the lock. Leave the Line alone as long as it leaves us alone. |
| **Cutters** | **Nadia Sobczak** | The Line will come back unless someone kills it. Cut every cable, sink every node, burn the Crib. They have been cutting Compact relight cables for years (the region's "copper thieves"). Sigrun Dahl at Pointe Sombre is theirs. |
| **Stewards** | **Wes Tolliver**, Kowalczyk's deputy and likely successor | If the Line must be controlled, let it be us, because we would never use it. Seize the Crib and hold it shut. (The contradiction is the point, and the Hands say so.) |

### Material resources
**Power**: the region's only working hydro plant, and the only **black-start-capable** source (a hydro station can restart without grid power; the Line needs one to restart). **Industry**: a foundry, a machine shop, the region's only working lathe, steel and parts. **The lock**. An **electrified infirmary** (sterilizer, lights, the region's best surgery). **A pre-collapse copy of the priority tables** (the Table Day printout and more). Two **section keys**: their own powerhouse node key, and the Pointe Sombre key Liv traded them.

### Economic role
Exports power as **charged battery cells** carried by boat (power moves only when someone carries it), steel, parts, and repairs. Charges **lock tolls**, which mirror the Compact's light dues and which the Local does not like to have pointed out.

### Culture, symbols, visual language
- A **gloved hand on a valve wheel**, and the numeral **9**.
- Hard hats painted with family lineage colors. Shift bells. Everything written in the **hand log** on boards by the gates.
- Members are **cardholders**; "Show your card" is both a check and a greeting. Solidarity is fierce and generous inside; outsiders wait at the gate.
- Not scavengers, not a militia: a town that works for a living and remembers being switched off.

### What ordinary members believe
The Line took their jobs and then took the lights. Outsiders who want the Line back want to run Aubin from somewhere else again. The Compact is a landlord with boats. The Sounding is strange but harmless.

### What leadership believes
Kowalczyk believes any system that can switch off a town from forty miles away will, eventually, be used to. He also knows the islands are dying slowly, and that his lock tolls help.

### What they are right about
**Control is the danger, and the tables are the catastrophe.** They are the only faction that has **lived** under the tables and the only one that knows concretely what an automatic black start would do. They prevent the disaster the others underestimate.

### What they are dangerously wrong about
- **Isolation is killing the islands**, and the Local's tolls are part of it.
- The Cutters sabotage lights that **save lives**.
- They are **a concentrated power** themselves: one town holding the only dam and the only lock.
- Destroying the Line **does not end the Current**, and it would end weather warnings, the relit lights, and any chance of reconnection for medicine.
- The Stewards' "hold it shut" is the Compact's plan with better manners.

### What they want from the Line
Hands: nothing. Cutters: its death. Stewards: its keys.

### If they get it
Cutters: the Line is gone; the lakes stay islanded for good; the Local's dam becomes the only power that matters. Stewards: the Line sits locked in Aubin's hands, and Aubin quietly becomes the Authority it hated.

### Why reasonable people join
Work, dignity, power in the lights and the infirmary, a vote. They kept a town alive for sixty years by hand.

### Why reasonable people fear them
They can close the river. The Cutters. A town that owns the only dam can decide who has a winter.

## 4. Interdependence

### 4.1 Who holds what

| Resource | Compact | Sounding | Local |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ships and convoys | **yes** | | |
| Fuel oil | **yes** | | needs it (lubricants, foundry) |
| Food and grain | **yes** | needs it (Tall Masts cannot feed itself) | needs it (Aubin imports grain) |
| Medicine (imported) | **yes** | | |
| Surgery and sterilizing | | | **yes** (electrified infirmary) |
| Route charts | **yes** | node maps | |
| Storm forecasts | needs them | **yes** | |
| Radio operators | needs them | **yes** | |
| Power, black-start capable | | | **yes** |
| Industry and parts | needs it | | **yes** |
| Passage between the lakes | needs it | | **yes** (the lock) |
| Protocol (the Lexicon) | | **partial** | |
| Section keys | copies from relit lights | the Tall Masts node | powerhouse key + Pointe Sombre key |
| The priority tables (knowledge) | | | **yes** |

### 4.2 Each prevents a catastrophe the others underestimate

- **The Local prevents the stale-table black start.** Only they know what the tables do. The Compact thinks the Line is neutral; the Answerers think it wants to speak.
- **The Sounding prevents a botched Severance.** Only they know the **stand-down sequence**: a procedure, not a password. Each section's breaker in the Crib is thrown in order while the operator speaks that section's call and waits for the Line's reply, so the Line records an orderly stand-down instead of a failure. It takes two people and about twenty minutes. A crude Cutter demolition at the Crib would trigger the Line's firebreak logic, which would island every section including Aubin's powerhouse, in winter.
- **The Compact prevents slow death by isolation.** The Local and the Readers both treat "leave it alone" as safe. It is not: people on the small islands die every winter for want of what ships carry.

### 4.3 Each is right about one thing, holds something the others cannot replace

| | Right about | Holds what others cannot replace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compact | Isolation kills | Ships, food, medicine, fuel |
| Sounding | It reacts; reconnection is not neutral | Forecasts, operators, protocol |
| Local | Control is the danger; the tables are a catastrophe | Black-start power, the lock, industry, the tables |

### 4.4 The black start needs all three

To restart the Line safely the player (or anyone) needs: **the Local's hydro** to black-start, **the Sounding's protocol** to operate the Crib without triggering a firebreak, **Compact ships** (or the Local's pumps) to get people and parts through to the Crib, and **the Local's copy of the tables plus Liv's census** to rewrite them. A player who has burned a faction must replace what it held by force, theft, or skill, at a visible cost (see `ENDINGS.md`).

### 4.5 The player as connector (cross-faction quests)

The player becomes the only person able to connect systems nobody controls alone. Seeds (developed in `QUEST_ARCS.md`):

- **Forecast Contract**: put a Sounding listener aboard a Compact convoy the Board does not trust, through a storm.
- **Cells for Grain**: broker a winter trade of Local battery cells for Compact grain, past a Cutter embargo.
- **The Lock Clause**: negotiate, break, or bypass the Local's lock tolls for an Open Water convoy.
- **Table Day**: show the Compact the Local's printout, or show the Local the Compact's Loss Book. Each changes what the other will agree to.
- **The Pointe Sombre Key**: the key Liv traded can end up with the Local, the Compact, the Sounding, the player, or back in its light. Whoever holds it at the end holds a vote.

## 5. Other powers (small, independent)

- **The Packet (the Post)**: the mail boats. Sworn to carry anything sealed for anyone and to read nothing. Neutral by oath, trusted by all three factions, owned by none. Captain **Odile Marchetti** (see `CHARACTERS.md`). The Post matters to the endgame: it can carry keys or evidence to every harbor at once.
- **Island settlements**: Pointe Sombre and others. Not factions: places that factions compete over.
- **Wreckers**: families who profit from dark reefs and, sometimes, show false lights. Local color with a moral edge. Not a faction.
- **Kenning's squatters**: a community in the drowned city's upper floors. The table demonstration happens to them.
- **Anishinaabe and Haudenosaunee nations**: this region is their homeland, and in any plausible post-collapse Great Lakes they are present, governing, and likely among the most resilient communities, with long-standing positions on water. **Deliberately not designed in this session.** They must not be reduced to a mystical explanation of the Current or a fourth philosophical faction written from outside. See `STORY_REVIEW.md`, Decision 10.

## 6. Reputation hooks (for the systems already in the game)

Faction standing would use the existing condition language (`Faction.` tag root is reserved in `technical_architecture.md`). Examples, not a spec:

- **Compact**: convoy berths, dues waived, access to the Loss Book and the Lights Office, Hale's trust. Negative: dues doubled, convoys refuse the player, the Warden's crews treat the player as a wrecker.
- **Sounding**: vigils, the Lexicon, forecasts (a practical item: a sounding that warns of the next storm), the *Tern* Book. Negative: listeners go silent when the player enters a hut; no forecasts.
- **Local**: lock passage, infirmary, charged cells, the Table Day printout. Negative: the lock closes to the player; the Cutters mark the player's boat.
- Reputation should often change **who is physically present** and **what is open**, not just prices.
