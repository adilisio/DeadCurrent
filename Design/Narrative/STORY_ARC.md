## 2. The spine in one paragraph

The player comes ashore at an abandoned Maritime Authority station looking for **Liv Kallio**, their older sister, a *listener* (someone who reads the Current's transmissions for weather and sells the warnings to captains). Liv followed a pattern in the transmissions to this shore, then to a lighthouse she deliberately darkened, then through the locks, then to the listeners' station, and finally out to an offshore water-intake crib four miles off a drowned city, where the pre-collapse network's central continuity node still stands. The player follows her by the same methods she used: cross bearings, old records, and people who remember her. Along the way the player learns that the collapse was made permanent by a deliberate act (the **Severance**, when the network was cut into islands), that the severed sections are slowly re-knitting themselves, and that if the network rejoins on its own it will run the region by priority tables sixty years out of date. Liv is alive. She has been sitting in the Crib for weeks, refusing the network's rejoin requests one by one by hand, like a switchboard operator refusing calls, because she believes no one's tables are fit to run the lakes, including the three factions who are now racing to reach her. The player reaches her knowing a great deal about the machine and the politics, and much less about the thing speaking through it, and has to decide what happens to the network, to Liv, and to the people who depend on both.

The player's question moves through three stages, matching `LongTermPlan.txt`'s aim:

1. **Prologue and Act I: "What happened here?"** (to this shore, this boat, this light, my sister)
2. **Act II: "What is happening now?"** (the network is re-knitting; everyone is moving; Liv's voice is on the air)
3. **Act III and Endgame: "What am I willing to do about it?"**

## 3. The region (PROPOSAL; fictional composite)

A fictional stretch of the upper Great Lakes: a river junction between two lakes and the archipelago around it. It borrows the *kind* of place the St. Marys River, the Straits, and the northern Huron islands are, without reproducing any real town. Whether to anchor it to a real area is an Anthony decision (`STORY_REVIEW.md`).

Locals call the two lakes **Upper** and **Lower**. The river and its islands are **the Narrows**. Only one lock still lifts ships between Upper and Lower.

| Place | What it is | Act |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Authority Shore** | The existing boathouse, shore path, scavenger camp, Mara's lookout, and the *Tern*. The far west edge of the Narrows, on Upper. | Prologue |
| **Pointe Sombre** | A small island settlement under a lighthouse on a reef-guarded point, a night's sail east. The vertical slice. | I |
| **Port Carrow** | The largest living harbor in the region, on Carrow Island. Walled breakwater, relit lights, market, the Compact's Board. | II |
| **Aubin Locks and Powerhouse** | A river town at the only working lock between Upper and Lower, beside a hydro dam that has run by hand for sixty years. The Local. | II |
| **Tall Masts** | A high island with a pre-collapse navigation transmitter station: guyed masts, a transmitter hall, listening huts. The Sounding. | II |
| **Kenning** | A drowned lake city on the far shore of Lower. Streets under water to the second storey. The Authority's regional headquarters tower, a pumping station, and the intake tunnel. | III |
| **The Four-Mile Crib** | A round stone water-intake crib four miles off Kenning, visible from the drowned waterfront. A light on it has flashed the same irregular rhythm for sixty years. The network's central continuity node. | III / Endgame |

Side-story places (see `QUEST_ARCS.md`): a flooded resort hotel, a salt mine under the lake bed, a pre-collapse automatic ferry that still runs its schedule, an ice road, a lock chamber that has held a freighter for sixty years.

**Why this cannot be transplanted.** The plot turns on lighthouse characteristics, light dues, cross bearings, lock passage, hydro black start, a water-intake crib and its tunnel under the lake bed, lake-effect and November gales, cold water that keeps the drowned, and a freshwater shipping economy. Remove the lakes and the plot does not work.

## 4. The buried network (author-level truth; the player learns most of this, not all)

This section is the writers' reference. It describes the **machine**, not the Current. What the Current is stays unexplained even here (see `MYSTERY_LEDGER.md` §9, Author constraints).

### 4.1 The Authority and the Line

- The **Great Lakes Maritime Authority** (PROVISIONAL name, in game) ran shipping, locks, aids to navigation, and storm protocol across the lakes.
- In the decades before the collapse, worsening storms and a fragile grid led the Authority to build the **Lakewide Continuity Network**. Its staff called it **the Line**, and the name survived. It linked lighthouses, locks, pumping stations, hydro plants, weather buoys, radio relays, and hardened **storm vaults** (emergency rooms under lighthouses and breakwaters) through **lake-bed cable** and radio. The central continuity node sat in a decommissioned water-intake crib off Kenning, reached by the old intake tunnel.
- The Line's job was **continuity of operations**: if people could not run the region in an emergency, it would. It shed load, closed locks, reordered power by **priority tables**, broadcast warnings, called ships to shelter, and could **island** the grid: cut it into self-sufficient sections so a failure could not cascade.
- "**Current events**" was public jargon before the collapse (the in-game storm-protocol notice): advisories for surges of induced current along shorelines and cables during geomagnetic storms. Live water, burnt relays, dead fish. It was a hazard warning, like a flood warning.

### 4.2 What the Authority kept quiet

- For roughly a decade before the collapse, the Line's own sensors recorded that some Current events had **structure**: a regular spacing, later called **the pattern**, in cable surges, sounder returns, and radio noise. A three-person office, the **Pattern Desk**, studied it. Their memos (found in Act III) argue the same three positions today's factions hold: *feedback in our own system*, *correspondence with something*, *an intrusion we must firewall*. The present-day factions are replaying an argument the Authority never finished.
- Survey launches like the *Tern* were sent to measure the pattern at cable junctions on the lake bed. "The mark off the point" on the *Tern*'s sounder is such a junction: **NOT A SHOAL**.
- The Desk's last recommendation was a **firebreak**: if the pattern ever began to move through the Line on its own, cut the Line into sections and lock them.

### 4.3 The Severance

- On the collapse night, the Line was cut into sections and each section's link locked. This is **the Severance**. It is why the collapse became permanent: the lights did not come back because the system that would have brought them back was deliberately cut.
- The command was entered at the Crib at 03:12 under the code of **Controller Maren Holt** (PROPOSAL name). The Crib's night crew had evacuated at 01:00. Holt's launch was found capsized at 02:30, forty minutes before her code was used. Her body was never found. The lake keeps its dead.
- **Who severed the lakes is never settled.** The evidence supports three readings: Holt reached the Crib after all; someone else used her code; or the Line's own emergency logic used stored authority (the Local calls it "a dead man's switch with no dead man"). See the Ledger.
- **Whether the Severance saved the region or doomed it is also never settled.** The firebreak may have stopped a worse cascade; it certainly made recovery impossible for sixty years.

### 4.4 Sections, keys, and resync

- The Line is divided into numbered **sections**. Each severed section's link is locked by a physical **section key** (PROPOSAL: a heavy, keyed exchange card seated in the section's storm vault) and a spoken **protocol** (call-and-response phrases on the section's channel, a pre-collapse way to operate infrastructure hands-free in storms).
- Severed sections run a **resync routine**: when a section's node has power and quiet, it asks to rejoin. For sixty years almost nothing had power. Now two things are powering nodes: the Current's own storms (induced current, the live battery on the *Tern*), and the **Compact relighting lighthouses** and fitting ships with salvaged Authority transponders. Every relit light is a node asking to rejoin.
- When enough sections rejoin, the Line will attempt an automatic **black start**: restart itself from the one black-start-capable source left in the region (the Aubin hydro plant) and run the lakes by its **priority tables**.
- **The tables are sixty years stale.** They power hospitals that are flooded, pump districts no one lives in, hold lake-level gates at pre-collapse targets, and have never heard of any settlement founded since. An automatic black start would misallocate power, close the lock under storm protocol, and drain or flood communities built on the new shorelines. That is the concrete, regional catastrophe: not the end of the world, but the Line faithfully restoring a world that is gone.
- The protocol and the keys are **how people can intervene**: refuse a resync, rewrite a table, split the keys, burn the Line, or open it.

### 4.4b Where the Crib gets its power (PROPOSAL)

A sixty-year-old console needs power. Proposal: the intake tunnel was fitted with a small **reversible turbine** for emergency supply. The lake's **seiche** (the whole basin sloshing slowly back and forth over hours) pushes water in and out of the four-mile tunnel, and the turbine turns with it. The Crib runs, faintly, on the lake breathing, plus induced current in the trunk cables during storms. This gives the Surveyors a strong argument (the Crib's activity rises and falls with the seiche because its **power** does) without explaining the pattern elsewhere (the *Tern*'s mast lamp, the fish ring, children on islands with no cable). It also gives the Intake dungeon a moving hazard: the water in the tunnel runs one way, stops, and runs back.

### 4.5 What the Line is not

- It is not conscious by design, not a villain, not an AI that "woke up". It is emergency automation with stale instructions and a lot of copper under the lakes.
- It is **not the Current.** The Current behaves differently where the Line exists (it carries further, louder, into machines) but appears where the Line does not (the *Tern*'s unwired mast lamp, the fish ring, sleepless children on islands with no cable). See the Ledger.

### 5.1 Options considered

### 5.2 Working version: A, with E's guilt folded in (PROPOSAL, not canon)

**Liv Kallio**, the player's older sister, is chosen for the draft because it gives the clearest reason to walk into danger on the first night, and because the story's work goes into making her *not* a rescue objective. The relationship itself is an open decision (`STORY_REVIEW.md`, Decision 1); most beats survive if she becomes a mentor, partner, or player-chosen relationship instead.

- **Who she is.** Mid-thirties. A **listener**: she reads the Current's transmissions aboard the Compact trader *Ida Lamberton* and sells storm warnings to captains. Listeners are useful and distrusted: sailors believe that repeating the words on a boat brings the storm (Mara's "don't repeat them on the boats"). Liv is brilliant at the work, impatient with superstition, and has never once asked permission.
- **Why the player cares.** She half-raised the player on the *Ida* after their mother was lost on the water. Guilt: two years before the game, the player salvaged an Authority tape from a wreck and gave it to Liv as a curiosity. On it, under the storm traffic, was the pattern. That tape is where this started.
- **What she was investigating.** The pattern precedes storms by a regular interval, which made her the best listener on the lakes. Then she noticed that the transmissions read out **present-day vessel names and positions**, including the *Ida*'s. Something on the Line is still watching the lakes, and reporting to no one.
- **Why she came here.** She followed bearings: the pattern is strongest at old cable junctions. The Authority Shore's junction, "the mark off the point", is where the *Tern* was sent sixty years ago.
- **What she discovered, in order.** The *Tern*'s mark is a junction (prologue). The lighthouse at Pointe Sombre is a section node trying to rejoin (Act I). The Local holds power and the lock (Act II). The Sounding holds the protocol (Act II). The Crib holds the tables, the Severance record, and the resync queue (Act III).
- **Choices she made before disappearing, and what they cost:**
  1. Seated a coil in the Authority Shore relay to listen from the boats (the Relay Ear reading: "seated by someone who knew the pinout"). The scavenger found it and kept it powered, and "things come to a signal like that".
  2. At Pointe Sombre, pulled the lighthouse's **section key** to stop the node rejoining. The light went dark. She told the keeper to relight it by hand. The clockwork was seized. The keeper's son **drowned** on the reef in the first storm. Liv had already sailed.
  3. Took Compact money (Warden Hale hired her to find the pattern's source) and stopped reporting to him.
  4. **Traded the Pointe Sombre key to the Local** for passage through Aubin Locks: the people most afraid of the Line now hold a piece of it, because of her.
  5. At Tall Masts, worked out enough of the **protocol** from the Sounding's archive to operate a section, and left with two Answerers. One of them died in the Kenning intake tunnel.
  6. At the Crib, **refused to let the Line black-start** and began rewriting the tables herself from a census of settlements she has been compiling for a year.
- **Why communication stopped.** Deliberately. Her last letter (the player carries it at the start) says *don't come*. She let the circuit believe she drowned, so that Hale would stop following her. The player's search is what leads others to her.
- **Is she alive?** Yes (working version). Exhausted, half-deaf from the hum, not sleeping, and very sure of herself.
- **Evidence she leaves** (a thread through every act; see `QUEST_ARCS.md`, Liv arc): her letters; "HEAR IT TOO" under the scavenger's KEEP OUT (her hand; PROPOSAL reinterpretation of existing text); the coil seating; pencil cross bearings on her charts; her note to the keeper at Pointe Sombre; her confiscated gear at the Locks; her annotations in the Sounding's archive; a **dead letter** held by the Packet; her census notebook; and, from mid-game, **her voice** in the transmissions.
- **How her story intersects the region.** Her actions caused a death at Pointe Sombre, handed the Local a key, and, by holding the Crib, are the only reason the Line has not black-started. She is simultaneously the person who most endangered the region and the person currently protecting it, and she has made herself its unelected operator. Every faction's leader will say "she has no right". Every faction's leader wants her chair.
- **How the player's opinion is meant to move:** *lost* (prologue) → *reckless* (Act I, the drowned boy) → *manipulative* (Act II, the Compact money, the traded key, the dead Answerer) → *right about something nobody else sees* (midpoint, the resync) → *someone who has decided she alone should run the lakes* (Act III) → the player decides what they think.
- **What stops it being a rescue.** The rescue objective is in direct conflict with the regional stakes: if the player takes Liv home, no one is holding the Line (see Endings, "Bring Her Home"). And she may not want to be rescued. She will not hand the Line to anyone, the player included: "Nobody should be *given* this." (Deliberately not a succession offer; see `AUDITS.md` §1, Fallout 4.)

## 6. Act structure

Timing is a rough full-game estimate for a player doing most content. The vertical slice (Act I's core) is 60 to 90 minutes by itself.

### PROLOGUE — The Authority Shore (existing Phases 1–4 content, plus a framing)

- **Immediate goal:** find out where Liv went from this shore.
- **Geography:** the *Ida* puts the player ashore at dusk at the Authority landing (a framing beat, not yet built) and will not wait near a talking relay: "Three nights. Then we're at Pointe Sombre on the dues run, with or without you." The player wakes in the boathouse (existing). Boathouse, shore path, scavenger camp, Mara's lookout, the *Tern*. Later: the landing stage from the production pilot.
- **Important discoveries:** the relay talks (existing); the *Tern*'s pattern and that its chart is a **bearing** (existing, with Schematic Eye); "Current events" on the storm notice was once an ordinary public warning (existing text, reread); "HEAR IT TOO" is in Liv's hand (PROPOSAL; confirmed later by matching her letter).
- **NPC introductions:** Mara (existing), the scavenger (existing), Captain Ines Varga of the *Ida* (framing).
- **Faction exposure:** indirect. The *Ida* flies Compact pennants; Authority stencils are being repainted "COMPACT" on the crates; Mara's taboo hints at listeners.
- **Key quests:** Shore Watch (existing), the *Tern* (existing), **Posted From the Shore** (PROPOSAL: ask after Liv; Mara deflects, then, depending on trust and Persuasion, admits Liv came through, asked about the *Tern*, and sailed for Pointe Sombre).
- **Major decision:** Shore Watch's route (existing: kill or coil). Secondary: keep or give up the Sounder Chart (it matters in Act II).
- **Mystery reveal:** independent machines share one pattern, and the pattern points somewhere.
- **World-state consequence:** existing flags (relay recovered, scavenger dead or alive, wreck power cut). Callback: a living scavenger reappears at Pointe Sombre among the wreckers.
- **Missing-person thread:** Liv was here five months ago. She seated the coil. She showed interest in the *Tern*. She left east.
- **Pull forward:** the *Ida* returns in a rising storm. The *Tern*'s bearing and Mara's admission point at Pointe Sombre. Mara (PROPOSAL: the companion) comes too, for reasons she does not fully give.

### ACT I — Pointe Sombre: "The Wrong Characteristic" (the vertical slice; see `QUEST_ARCS.md` §2)

- **Immediate goal:** Liv's trail leads here; the settlement's light is dark.
- **Geography:** the crossing in a Current storm (the *Ida* nearly hits the reef because the light is out, and a false light is burning on the wrong headland); the settlement; the lighthouse; the storm vault under it; a wreckers' cove; two or three wilderness sites.
- **Important discoveries:** every light has a **characteristic** (its own flash signature, printed on charts). Before it went dark, this one had begun flashing the **pattern** instead of its own. Ships misread it; a laker hit the reef; the wreck's salvage started a small wrecking trade. In the vault: an Authority log recording **Current events decades before the collapse**, a node panel reading **SECTION 14 — SEVERED — RESYNC PENDING**, and a direction-finding loop that gives **bearing two**.
- **NPC introductions:** keeper **Odette Beaudry**; the wrecker family (the **Pruitts**); **Sigrun Dahl**, a Local lineworker "buying copper"; **Warden Casimir Hale**, Compact, come to relight the light for the dues route. A lone Sounding pilgrim may be passing (hint only).
- **Faction exposure:** the Compact and the Local, directly (the two initial-production factions). The Sounding by rumor.
- **Key quests:** **The Wrong Characteristic** (main), **False Light** (wreckers), **Copper Buyer** (Sigrun), **Loss Book** (Hale's ledger of ships lost), plus small encounters.
- **Major decision:** what happens to the light, decided in the vault and the lamp room: relight it on the Line, relight it by hand, redirect its power to the settlement, destroy the node, and separately what to expose (Liv, the keeper, the wreckers).
- **Mystery reveal:** the collapse did not simply happen to the Line; this node was **severed by an order**, and it is trying to rejoin.
- **World-state consequence:** the light's state (on the Line, by hand, dark, destroyed), night safety, the wreckers' fate, merchant stock, who keeps the light, Compact and Local reputation.
- **Missing-person thread:** Liv pulled the section key. The keeper's son drowned the week after. Liv's note to the keeper: *light it by hand; I'm sorry; don't let anyone put it back on the Line*. The player learns their sister caused a death and left.
- **Pull forward:** two bearings cross somewhere beyond the Narrows, too loose to fix. Liv went through **Aubin Locks** on the Packet. Hale will pay the player to find her; Sigrun will take the player to the Local.

### ACT II — The Crossings: "Whose Lights"

Three regions, loosely ordered. The player can visit Port Carrow and the Locks in either order; Tall Masts opens once the player has heard of it from either.

**Pacing rule (revised after the pacing audit, `AUDITS.md` §3):** each faction arc is split across the midpoint. Before "Kallio at the Crib" the player meets each faction, earns or loses its trust, and learns what it holds. The arcs' **decisions** (Kestrel Light, the Floor vote, the Vigil's aftermath, custody of the Pointe Sombre key) land **after** the broadcast, when every faction is racing for the Crib and each decision decides who comes. This puts the midpoint near the true middle of the game instead of about 70% through it, and makes Act III carry faction climaxes rather than only a dungeon. Each region is introduced by a **different verb** so Act II is not three trips to a relay: Port Carrow by **convoy** (sailing), the Locks by **passage** (negotiation or break-in), Tall Masts by **vigil** (a night of listening).

#### IIa. Port Carrow and the Aubin Locks

- **Immediate goal:** find out where Liv went past the Locks, and why both factions want her.
- **Discoveries:**
  - Port Carrow: the Compact's convoys carry salvaged Authority transponders; the Line reads them, which is how the transmissions know the *Ida*'s name. The Compact's **Loss Book** (every vessel lost for forty years) shows losses fall sharply where lights were relit, and that relit nodes are rejoining. Liv's contract with Hale, and her unpaid-back advance.
  - Aubin Locks: the Local's history (lock and powerhouse workers automated out by the Line, locked out on the collapse night, broke back in and saved the town). Liv's confiscated gear. The Local holds the **Pointe Sombre section key**: Liv traded it for passage.
  - At the lock: speaking a **protocol phrase** on the lock channel moves the gates. The taboo "don't repeat them on the boats" was folk memory of something real. And one of the lock's replies is not in any manual.
- **NPC introductions:** Commissioner **Aurelie Dumont** (Compact Board); Captain Varga again (Open Water wing); Chief Operator **Bram Kowalczyk** (the Local); **Nadia Sobczak** (the Cutters inside the Local).
- **Key quests:** **Light Dues** (Compact), **Hand Log** (Local), **Lock Passage** (main: get through the lock by permission, by convoy force, by protocol, or by stealth).
- **Major decision:** how the player passes the lock, and whether the Pointe Sombre key stays with the Local, goes to the Compact, or goes with the player.
- **Mystery reveal:** the Line **still watches** and still **obeys words**.
- **World-state consequence:** lock access for the rest of the game, convoy routes, Compact and Local standing; if the protocol was used, the Local knows the Line can override their gates, and the Cutters escalate.
- **Missing-person thread:** Liv is manipulative, not only reckless: she took money she never meant to earn and put a key in the hands of people she knew would never use it well.

#### IIb. Tall Masts

- **Immediate goal:** Liv spent weeks here; find what she learned and where she went.
- **Discoveries:** the Sounding's founders were the *Tern*'s two crew, who walked inland and wrote the rest of the log elsewhere (the *Tern* Book). The Sounding greet each other with "Again". Their archive holds **the Lexicon** (partial protocol) and sounder rolls going back sixty years; the pattern's base period matches the lake basin's **seiche** (the natural slosh of the whole lake, measured in hours). A contested wireless log from a much older November gale records "regular ticking", long before any Line existed. A **third bearing** from the masts closes the fix: **the Four-Mile Crib**.
- **NPC introductions:** First Reader **Abigail Tennant**; the Answerer **Callum Reyes**; Mourners bringing names to be read into the channel; children who cannot sleep in storms, taken in as apprentices.
- **Key quests:** **The Vigil** (a storm night of listening), **The *Tern* Book** (the player can return the Sounder Chart), **Mara's Book** (companion), **Dead Letter** (the Packet delivers Liv's undelivered letter to the player: *I'm not lost. I'm choosing.*).
- **Major decision:** what the player gives the Sounding and what they take: the chart, Mara's transcripts, the promise to carry a "reply" to the Crib.
- **Mystery reveal:** the pattern is coupled to the lakes' physics **and** changes when people speak on the channel. Both things are true.
- **Missing-person thread:** Liv left with two Answerers. From here, **her voice is in the transmissions.**

#### Midpoint — "Kallio at the Crib"

During a storm, every relay in the region, including the Authority Shore relay if it still has a coil, broadcasts **Liv's voice**: *"...every light you relight wakes a section. You're rebuilding their tables. I can't hold it by myself..."* Every faction hears it. Now everyone knows where she is. Hale mobilizes a Compact expedition; the Cutters move to burn the Crib; the Answerers move to join her. The player's private search becomes everyone's race.

Later, at the Crib, Liv says **she never broadcast it**. She said those words, alone, at night, inside the control room, to no one. Something put them on the air. The Ledger keeps three readings open.

### ACT III — Kenning: "The Tables"

- **Immediate goal:** reach the Crib before, or with, the people racing there.
- **Before Kenning (the race):** the faction arcs' decisions (see the pacing rule in Act II). Each one decides who sails for Kenning and in what mood: Board marines or Open Water captains, Cutters with charges or Stewards with a seizure crew, a Reader with a recorder or Answerers with the transmitter keys.
- **Geography:** the drowned city of Kenning (a Compact beachhead on the waterfront, a Cutter camp in the flooded rail yard, a squatter community on the upper floors). The Authority headquarters tower. The pumping station. **The intake tunnel**, four miles under the lake bed, partly flooded (the second dungeon type of the full game).
- **Important discoveries:** the **Pattern Desk** memos; the **Severance record** and Holt's code used forty minutes after her launch was found capsized; the **priority tables** themselves. A small demonstration: powering the pumping station (to reach the headquarters' lower floors) lets the Line reallocate power by its old table: it lights the drowned hospital's circuits and cuts the squatters' heat. The player watches the catastrophe at one-block scale.
- **NPC introductions:** Maren Holt's recorded voice (pre-collapse logs, one per location); the Kenning squatters' spokesperson; faction forces in the field under Hale, Sobczak, and Reyes.
- **Faction exposure:** all three in one place, in conflict, needing things only the others have (see `FACTIONS.md`, Interdependence).
- **Key quests:** **The Severance Record**, **Pumping Station**, **The Intake** (each faction's resources open a different route through the tunnel: the Local's power runs the pumps, a Compact boat takes the flooded shaft, the Sounding's protocol opens the bulkheads, or the player forces it with Engineering and explosives at a cost).
- **Major decision:** who the player brings through the tunnel. That decides who is physically present in the Crib.
- **Mystery reveal:** the collapse was a choice, possibly nobody's; the tables are the real catastrophe; the Current is not the Line.
- **World-state consequence:** Kenning's squatters powered or cut off; faction forces present, absent, or dead; tunnel state.
- **Missing-person thread:** the dead Answerer in the tunnel. Liv's census notebook in a relay room: her rewritten tables, and whose names she left off.

### ENDGAME — The Crib: "Black Start"

- **Reunion.** Liv at the resync console, refusing requests by hand. She is glad to see the player, and still says the player should not have come.
- **What the player learns last:** inside the Crib the pattern is strongest, and it **changes** when Liv refuses a request. Liv: *"It's asking."* The engineer in the room (Hale, Sigrun, or the player with Engineering): *"It's a retry loop with jitter."* Neither can be proved.
- **What the player decides, by doing** (see `ENDINGS.md`): black-start the Line under chosen tables; burn it; open it; split its keys; seize it for someone; or take Liv home and leave the Line to whoever comes next. Each is a physical act in the Crib (seat keys, rewrite a table at the console, set charges on the trunk cables, unlock the protocol governors, carry Liv out through the tunnel).
- **What the player cannot know:** what answering, silencing, or ignoring the pattern means for whatever is on the other end.

### EPILOGUE

Slides or short playable returns to visited places, driven by world state (light states, lock state, who lives, who holds keys), then one **last transmission** on the player's radio. Its content varies by ending and never explains.

## 8. Why the player matters (no prophecy)

The player is not chosen. They matter because:

- they **follow Liv's method** (bearings, records, people), which no faction is doing, because each faction is sure it already knows what the Line is;
- they **can move between factions** that cannot talk to each other (trader background, a sister who burned every bridge, no oath to anyone);
- they **gather access**: the keys, the Lexicon, the lock, the tunnel routes, the census, each earned through a quest;
- they **decide who stands in the Crib**, because they decide who comes through the tunnel;
- they **know the places**: in the census-table endings, communities the player found and recorded are on the tables, and the ones they never visited are not (see `ENDINGS.md` §3). Exploration has ending weight.
