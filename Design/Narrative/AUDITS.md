**What reads as DEAD CURRENT's own:** a collapse made permanent by **islanding**; a missing Liv **holding a switchboard** four miles offshore; **stale priority tables** as the catastrophe; lighthouse **characteristics**, **light dues**, and a **Loss Book**; a union town that runs the only **black-start** dam by hand; listeners founded by a **survey crew** who greet each other with "Again"; a **Shore Watch** whose keepers inherit its memory and speak it as "I"; and a finale in a **water-intake crib**. None of these transplants to Boston, the Mojave, or Appalachia.

## 2. Thematic Audit

What the story is about under the plot. Four tensions:

| Tension | In one line |
| --- | --- |
| **T1. Connection vs isolation** | Being connected keeps people alive and makes them controllable; being cut off makes them free and lets them die. The Line literalizes it: interconnection vs islanding. |
| **T2. Stewardship vs control** | Everyone who wants to protect the Line wants to hold it. Holding something "so no one misuses it" is how misuse starts. |
| **T3. Old instructions** | Rebuilding an old system rebuilds its old priorities. The tables, the Pattern Desk's argument, Table Day, the ferry keeping its schedule: the past keeps running. |
| **T4. Listening before acting** | Understanding something does not give you the right to use it, and you never get to understand it fully before you must act. ("People who still listen before they shoot.") |

Are they dramatized, or only stated? Checked across the draft:

| | T1 Connection / isolation | T2 Stewardship / control | T3 Old instructions | T4 Listening / acting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Factions** | Compact (connect) vs Local (stay islanded) | Stewards; Dumont; Hale; the Local's tolls mirroring light dues | The Compact's succession claim; the Local's Table Day | Readers vs Answerers |
| **Characters** | Liv cut herself off to protect everyone; Mara on her shore | Liv and Hale mirror each other; Odile holds without owning | June Okafor's sixty years of silence; Mara's inherited watch | Mara's refusal to interpret; Liv acting on "it's asking" |
| **Quests** | Lock Passage; Kestrel Light; Cells for Grain | Pointe Sombre key custody; the Floor vote | Pumping Station; The Schedule | The Vigil; the net loft exposure scene |
| **Locations** | The Narrows; the lock; Pointe Sombre | The Crib's key wall | Kenning: a city whose power still follows a dead city's plan | Tall Masts' listening huts |
| **Endings** | Black Start vs Severance | Keys Divided vs Crown a Keeper | Old tables vs census tables | Open Channel; every ending's unknowns |

**Gap found and addressed:** T2 needed an object the player must decide to hold or give away. **The Pointe Sombre key** now does that across Acts I–III (custody is a decision in the consequence table), and the **Post** is the counter-example: connection that nobody controls, by oath.

**Gap left for Anthony:** T4 lands hardest if the player sometimes **acts too soon and pays for it** in a small, early way. The Vigil reply is one example. The slice could use another (for example, relinking the light before reading the vault's log shows the pattern in the lamp that same night). Suggested, not added.

**The title:** a *dead current* is current in a line that should be dead; it is the lake's current; and, to the Mourners, it is the dead's voices. The story uses all three and confirms none.

## 6. Sensitivity notes

- **Indigenous nations.** The upper Great Lakes are Anishinaabe (Ojibwe, Odawa, Potawatomi) and Haudenosaunee homelands. A post-collapse Great Lakes without them would be false, and a version that uses their spirituality to "explain" the Current would be harmful and a cliché. This session **did not design** Indigenous characters or communities. Recommendation: include them as present, governing communities with their own interests in water and fishing rights, written with consultation or a sensitivity review, and never as the mystery's answer. (`STORY_REVIEW.md` Decision 10.)
- **Real disasters and songs.** The November gales and lost lake freighters are real history with living families. Use the **tradition** ("the lake keeps its dead", the November reading) and fictional ships; do not quote song lyrics or name real wrecks.
- **Children.** The storm-waking children and the vigils involve harm to children by adults who believe they are helping. Handle it seriously, without spectacle; the game never shows a child hurt on screen.
